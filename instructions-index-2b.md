# Rule index, part 4 of 4: Case patterns (K)

One line per rule: ID, [trigger], summary, (file). Read all four parts in full on every pass (instructions.md, step 3).

### 11. Case patterns (K)
How to use: general patterns live in the sections above; a K entry is the specific situation where one kind of business used a pattern, written in general words, with its linked rules naming where the pattern is held. Match the business's model, stage and constraint against each trigger; use a match as an analogy marked "Proposed, not decided", never as proof. Rules in other sections win over K.
- K1 [a local service business with walk-in or inbound traffic but low conversion of enquiries] Scripted first contact, short fixed-length challenge programme as entry offer, membership sold at the end while the result is visible. (rules-cases.md)
- K2 [an owner whose one business works and peers ask how] License or teach the proven system to owners of the same kind of business, only with measured, provable results. (rules-cases.md)
- K3 [a fragmented local market of many small operators and an owner with capital] Buy or merge operators in one area onto one system, brand and back office; later stages only; competition law check. (rules-cases.md)
- K4 [many small buyers in one trade each paying full price for the same supplies] Run a buying group that pools volume for better terms, paid by fee or share of saving; never price fixing. (rules-cases.md)
- K5 [a core product that can be free or at cost, with a paired paid service] Free core to widen the funnel, earn on the paired service most users need; check cost per free user and cash. (rules-cases.md)
- K6 [a low-price job sold door to door or in person, needed again] Win the first job, bundle repeats into a monthly membership so lifetime value pays for the selling; off-premises contract rules. (rules-cases.md)
- K7 [a low-price automated online sale where some buyers can afford much more] Call team contacts recent automated buyers and offers the higher next step; lawful channel only. (rules-cases.md)
- K8 [an agency or service business with constant demand but churn, low prices and labour-heavy delivery] Many vendors for a universal need signals low stickiness; rebuild delivery with checked AI, price consistently, keep clients longer; the advantage lasts while good AI is hard. (rules-cases.md)
- K9 [a generalist professional service competing on price] Pick one client group passing the market tests, publish free practical content on their wider business goals from cross-client insight, sell the core service as the way there. (rules-cases.md)
- K10 [a business whose audience has seen many kept promises, launching a new product] Launch to that audience first, take orders in arrival order, real queue only, full terms before payment. (rules-cases.md)
- K11 [a brand pairing itself with a figure, cause or campaign its core buyers dislike] Cautionary: good advertising, bad branding; count who a pairing gains and loses before running it. (rules-cases.md)
- K12 [selling a good new product to a shrinking industry] Cautionary: a good offer can't outrun a shrinking market; serve the growing side or pick a growing market. (rules-cases.md)
- K13 [a service giving a percentage gain, sold to small clients] Sell the same work to much larger clients and price on the value; check work quality, then who it's sold to, before blaming sales. (rules-cases.md)
- K14 [a creative or expert service where the idea is valued above the making] Sell the concept or diagnosis as the paid product; price execution separately or hand it off with a brief. (rules-cases.md)
- K15 [a seasonal service business profitable in one season and losing or laying off in the other] Accept the swing; one service per season with the same crews, never "off season"; fix the losing season's sales then price; keep the line with the better ratio. (rules-cases.md)
- K16 [a high-end local practice whose wealthy clients share clubs and communities] Client-hosted events at their clubs spread through peers at almost no cost; referral built into every visit; give away instead of discounting. (rules-cases.md)
- K17 [a project service with improvised prices, thin margins and reps closing worse than the owner] Quote formula, price lock, prepaid and multi-year terms, reps set and owner closes, same-day quotes, industry events. (rules-cases.md)
- K18 [a family practice where one senior practitioner does all the core work] Hire practitioners, senior moves to teaching, raise prices on a very high close rate, more cash up front. (rules-cases.md)
- K19 [a cash-strapped business running a big giveaway] Cautionary: thousands of leads unconverted; set follow-up capacity before the surge. (rules-cases.md)
- K20 [an expert coaching business with the owner as bottleneck and long pitches] Team answers repeat questions, short weekly webinar, proper one-to-one price, upsell part-way through the programme. (rules-cases.md)
- K21 [an outlet owner who also coaches owners in the same trade, asked to do the work for them] Sell the outlets to focus, lead with a fixed-term done-for-you build then continuity, coaching as front end; online product sales with a service voucher as the acquisition method, lawfully. (rules-cases.md)
- K22 [a consulting service with organic attention whose booked buyers don't understand the offer] Pre-call video and pre-framing questions, good friction, a fast-cash campaign from the client's own lists in onboarding, action-tied bonuses and guarantee. (rules-cases.md)
- K23 [an owner or investor about to invest after working closely with a company] Cautionary: months of close work hid a fake team and two sets of books; run the four goods before any money moves. (rules-cases.md)
- K24 [a specialist with a tiny niche following selling low-price products or booking calls] A narrow, exact audience converts despite few likes; personal next-step emails to every low-price buyer. (rules-cases.md)
- K25 [a buyer who finds the seller of a business defrauded customers before the sale] Cautionary: due diligence and indemnities before signing; after the fact, weigh recovery against years of cost, often outgrow the loss instead. (rules-cases.md)
- K26 [a small retailer of others' brands losing shoppers to online after they try in store] Make the in-store piece one of one, the visit an experience, carry the sizes asked for, paid fitting credited; long-term own or exclusive products. (rules-cases-b.md)
- K27 [a profitable hobby membership sold through paid live challenges, capped by fear of ad spend] Annual only with a physical kit, monthly after cart close, hundreds of ads from members and clips, spend most at the end, limit from lifetime gross profit, test a paid trial. (rules-cases-b.md)
- K28 [a profitable local home service with high close and show rates, one referral partner and ads to an unoptimised page] Price rise with break-even close rate, dedicated ad page, phone-size ad checks, spend after fixes, branded search, monthly reactivation, partners sell the entry check, community-event outbound. (rules-cases-b.md)
- K29 [a door-to-door sales team moved onto bought leads that cost more than a client is worth] Back to outbound, inbound to the best closer as a promotion, one seller to iterate, an in-house marketer. (rules-cases-b.md)
- K30 [an online store with standard and custom products, one dominant ad platform and broken tracking] Fix attribution first, focus on the least price-sensitive buyers, custom route to a call on every page, twice-weekly list emails. (rules-cases-b.md)
- K31 [an event-based training business with many one-day events, a few profitable multi-day camps and a copycat] Cut weak one-day dates, scale the camps, raise prices, let the copycat take the low-margin format. (rules-cases-b.md)
- K32 [an information business with a cheap book funnel, one ad platform, stalled spend and slow payback] Customer content loop with consent, variations of the winning creative, lead scoring and a bigger calling team, trade-offs first on calls. (rules-cases-b.md)
- K33 [a founder-delivered coaching business maxed out on time, with a low-price tier and costly multi-day challenges] One high offer in small groups plus events, no unlimited messaging, quarter up front, one long live session, renewals in person. (rules-cases-b.md)
- K34 [a local capital-heavy subscription service losing money, with tendered and individual buyers] Individuals only, door-to-door as the one channel, first transaction rebuilt so blended first cash beats costs, either-or with yearly prepay. (rules-cases-b.md)
- K35 [a high-end personal service to wealthy individuals with a leaky opt-in page and a few partners bringing much of the revenue] Fix the page first, ten times the partner outreach to adjacent businesses, then a continuity anchor, continuity as the default next step and steady price rises. (rules-cases-b.md)
- K36 [a multi-location appointment service with co-owning partners, weak rebooking and staff turnover] Settle the partnership first, speed to lead, peak pricing with member exemption, rebook and membership before the service, prepay at checkout, career path. (rules-cases-b.md)
- K37 [a busy high-volume walk-in business, such as a restaurant, preparing a second location] Fix the first unit's per-order economics (price endings, menu, add-on scripts, comeback and review asks) before opening the next. (rules-cases-b.md)
- K38 [a niche professional service with a content audience losing money across several lines] Cut the losing line, point all asks at the route that already sells, and sell a fixed outcome package at service margins. (rules-cases-b.md)
- K39 [a consultancy selling a large project to strangers from cold ads] Sell a small, fast first step on one call, then the main project at the first result, with the first price credited. (rules-cases-b.md)
- K40 [a consumable subscription bootstrapped from pre-sales] Pre-sell, convert first buyers by phone, use "for as long as you stay" bonuses and recurring promoter commissions, outsource all but the critical input. (rules-cases-b.md)
- K41 [a practice paid by third-party payers whose payments are falling] Choose network efficiency or private payment; for private, one to three entry offers, a small paid assessment, sell at the end of it. (rules-cases-b.md)
- K42 [a low-margin made-to-order products business with repeat and impulse buyers] Close losing locations, price-test, max out the working search channel, go all in on short-form for impulse gifts, add repeat-purchase offers and email. (rules-cases-b.md)
