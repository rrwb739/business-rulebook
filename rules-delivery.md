## Delivery and operations (D)

- D1: moved to X4.
- D2: moved to X5.
- D3 [planning or running client sessions that change a client's workflows] [shape: service] Think in workflows, not roles: list each client staff member's tasks in the area the offer covers, then automate one workflow end to end before the next. Before automating, ask each staff member who will run it where it breaks now and what they would change, and bring them into questioning and deleting steps, so the version they run is partly theirs. Then go in this order: question each step (why, for whom, what output), delete what does not serve the output, simplify, speed up, then automate; never automate a step that should have been deleted. Roll each new workflow onto a small batch first (for example 10 cases), check quality (errors, complaints, outputs received), then widen. Messages the client sends its own customers give a choice (two routes, two dates) with a one-line reason rather than a bare demand, are accurate on any rule they cite (X4), and are tested against demand wording on that batch, measured on the response the workflow needs. Lead each such message with what the recipient gets, in five beats: show the client understands how the recipient works, the real consequence plainly, one specific action, an easier route, and thanks; any penalty or rule fact comes from official guidance and the client checks it (X4, O47). Never say how many recipients are late: where the client's records show it, say most have already responded, keep disapproval on the late act, never the person, and give the how-to and the recipient's benefit. Before automating, walk each workflow backwards once (from the final output to the first step, asking at each step what had to be true for it to happen on time; the owner's own funnel likewise, from paid invoice back to first message), and write down the one thing the current way does best so the new version keeps it. Every automated workflow has a named exceptions pile (a reply, upload or record it does not expect) handled by hand by a named person and counted weekly, with the commonest cause fixed in the checklist (D5), and a written manual fallback the client can run in its busiest week if a tool, integration or the owner's access fails, rehearsed once before the first live deadline. If a workflow error reaches any client's customers, pause that workflow in every client running it until the L75 review and checklist fix are done, telling each client the same day. All of this stays within X4 and X5.
- D4 [building prompts or AI drafting for a client] [shape: service] Have the AI ask its clarifying questions first, then train it like a new employee: written rules plus the client's best examples. Judge it against the human process given equal training time. Every AI-drafted message to the client's customers gets a named human check against a short checklist before it goes. In session one, tell staff that questioning or stopping any step that looks wrong, the owner's included, is part of the job, and praise it when they do (D13).
- D5 [designing or reviewing sessions, or training anyone] Success means the team can run it without the owner. Document, demonstrate, duplicate: record the task and write the checklist, then do the next work block from the checklist alone (if the result is still top quality, the draft holds); demonstrate it while they watch and fix the checklist wherever they stop the owner; then they do it in front of the owner. Before trainees run a workflow, show 2 or 3 recorded examples done right (X5) and ask them to talk through what they would do, plus one or two anonymised error cases on a made-up test case, each followed by "what would you do instead?" and the right step. Once clients consent, examples show people in a similar role at a similar-sized client, not only the owner (D13). Give each trainee one part of the method to learn first and teach back to the others, with the named lead checking all of it. Let the named lead choose within the fixed method (tone, timing, batch order, M13), and replace bare bans with the replacement step and its reason. The bar: could a stranger get the owner's results from the checklist alone? If they get it wrong, the checklist was confusing; a step that needs explaining should be split. Competence is not performance: plan reps (slow, smooth, fast). Judge whether they follow the directions, not the result: if they follow them and the result is wrong, the checklist is wrong. Praise each correct step, do not take over when they slip (unless a client's customer would be affected right now; then step in and debrief one to one, D16), give one piece of feedback at a time, and never punish mistakes in training. When performance dips, retrain, find the dropped step and reward following it. Before giving feedback, ask them to check the run against the checklist themselves, with the fix slot booked (D14); for any repeat task, set one timed target with a stop point, time each run, keep a list of missed steps to repeat, and point out every improvement. Any role suggested for client staff comes with a written scope, carrying one line on how it moves the client's own count, and the client appoints them. Done-with-you scales; done-for-you hits the owner's hours. Front-load training early, plan reps for the rest, and never promise full independence by a date unless pilots show it (O15). Never sell someone else's delivery at the owner's price until their recorded client results match the owner's; until then, ration the owner's access (group sessions, recordings). After any handoff, compare the numbers: if they fall, the owner abdicated rather than delegated, so work side by side until they hold. On every handoff (setter to closer, closer to delivery), introduce the next person by name and role, book their first contact, pass the notes, edify them, and stay the owner of it until they confirm contact; step back from calls or support gradually. Before the last week, hand reinforcement to the client's named lead: the short list of steps to praise when done and correct when skipped. Add every fix a client finds (X5) to the shared checklist so the next client gets it. Size the detail to the person: junior staff get step-by-step checklists with exact wording, senior people get the outcome and the check, and every checklist is written for the least experienced person who will run it. Ship every workflow as fill-in templates in the client's own branding and wording, with as few decisions as possible and one task per short video, and check the spread of pilot results, not just the average: a wide spread means a variable is still uncontrolled. Put each workflow's first step as a cue where staff do the task. Staff copy what the owner does more than what the owner says: before each intake, check the habits they will see. Before the first pilot, run each session aloud, timed, with an outsider, count teaching reps under F24, and after each real session note where staff stalled and whether the explanation caused it.
- D6 [delivery work that repeats across clients] Template it so each new client costs less to deliver.
- D7 [onboarding a new client] [shape: service] In the first 48 hours, make and keep small promises and say exactly what happens next. Paying only gives them permission to start: say so at sign-up, and get their first task done inside those 48 hours (D12). Within 24 hours of signing, send a short personal welcome video that thanks them for their trust and says the owner's job now is to justify it. Re-sell the destination at onboarding and at the first-week check-in. Test: offer nearby clients an in-person kickoff, within F1 hours, and compare referral and follow-on take-up with remote kickoffs. At kickoff, ask what the client will stop or pause for the programme and record it as an action with an owner (D14); ask the named lead to write down what result would make them the best case, predict out loud the tasks the team will complete and sessions they will attend, and share that goal with their colleagues; record it in their words and read it back at the first check-in. All are questions, never conditions or promises (M27, O15). Offer the named lead the role as the demanding job it is, with true stretch challenges; public dares stay out (M27). Tell staff where and how to ask between sessions, that small questions are welcome, and the owner's real reply time (O8), shown on every session slide and checklist footer; check each quarter that nothing sits between staff and that reply time (M9). Offer two support routes with the same reply time and let them pick; ask how the team handles a mistake today (fixed quietly, reported or blamed), logged in their words (C4), to set the D4 and D13 tone. Write down what the team expects from the owner, then what the owner can expect from them, beside the guarantee conditions; this adds to the written terms and never changes them (O8, X3). For group intakes, run the kickoff as one group session (still with the welcome video and first task in 48 hours), opening with a 2-minute round per client (location, team size, main problem this period; nothing on fees, customer lists or customer details, M12), each followed by one true, specific line from the owner's notes; compare first-task completion with one-to-one kickoffs. Give the most experienced staff member a reviewer or owner role (O28). At 48 hours, ask the decision maker "how do you feel about having signed?" (1 to 10), log it against the sales call recording and review any low score for pressure (M27, L75). Before kickoff, get the names and roles of everyone attending and use them. The named lead enters the session dates in their own calendar. Ask each staff member who will run a workflow what they want from it and tie their tasks to that answer (D12). If two people claim the same area, give each one named part to own (D14). Give each client a one-page map: the parts of the method in build order, one line on why each comes before the next, the named lead and weekly tasks, and what each side can expect, in the client's wording, labelled as the owner's own method (O25; see X9), pinned where the team works. Release the rest in weekly portions: at kickoff only the map, the guarantee conditions and week 1's checklist; the full terms still go before commitment (O8, X3). Spend 5 minutes before every client call or session rereading the notes, the kickoff goal, last actions and the previous outcome.
- D8 [measuring usage or client results] [shape: service] Ask specific count questions, not yes or no questions, at set points (day 1, 30, 60, 90 and the end; L32). At each check-in, note in the named lead's exact words whether they call the workflows "we" or "it"; a shift from "we" to "it" is an early warning to act on before renewal (M9), marked as an inference (C4). Track by workflow and the named lead's own tracker entries, never by monitoring individual staff (screen, keystroke or per-person activity logs); per-staff data is the client's decision as employer (see X8). Build triggers into the tracker for misses on the owner's side (a reply past the stated time, a cancelled session, a template error) and for a guarantee milestone missed while the client met the conditions; when one fires, tell the client the same day and apply the stated remedy before they claim it (O8, O42, L75). When a client's own account disagrees with its tracker, treat the measure as suspect first: check how it is counted against the source, re-measure at the next set point, log both, and never overrule the client with the number.
- D9 [deciding what to improve] Ask clients open, non-leading questions about what need it fills, and invest there. At each pilot review, ask what they value (let more of them get it, sooner), what they do not (cut it), and what they do not understand (reword it). Invite the 1 or 2 most engaged clients to be design partners, framed as the favour it really is, with any perk disclosed and never tied to a positive review (X3, L37). Collect feedback on a private form before any group discussion. With written consent, credit a design partner in a template's version notes, never used publicly to imply endorsement (O25). Tell each person who suggested a change what was decided and why, including when it was not used. At the first follow-on check-in after a real cycle of use, ask the named lead to rank the parts from most to least useful and name one thing missing. At each review and exit interview, ask what suppliers in the field usually do that annoys them and fix one where X3 and F1 allow. The owner reads every client and prospect message, giving any single surprising remark a dated "why is this happening?" check within a week, while product changes still wait for F27's three clients unless the check finds a fault. Tag every complaint as a result issue (they did not get what they wanted) or an experience issue (not the way they wanted it); a good experience never excuses a missed result (M9). At each review, ask "if you could keep only one thing, what would it be?". Ask clients who have stayed longest why they stay, and keep asking until one shared reason appears; that reason, not what the owner assumed, becomes the value line in O3, and making it truer becomes delivery's main goal (O29). Rank problems to solve by reach (how many suffer), impact (how much), confidence the fix works and cost, and set a fixed monthly time or money budget for the top-ranked client problem. The brand's promise stays fixed while the way it is kept keeps improving, since what buyers experience outweighs anything the business says (L65).
- D10 [the owner's own admin] If the business sells a method, use it on the owner's own admin first: the owner should be the first proof. Use the same D3 order: question, delete, simplify, speed up, then automate. Time how long logging one touch takes and cut it towards 30 seconds (one lead-sheet entry sets status, note and next touch due, L8); tools may queue and draft, never send unapproved (L7) or play recorded messages (X2). From the first paid sale, keep the business's books in proper accounting software and reconcile monthly.
- D11 [client acquisition starts working well] Delivery capacity becomes the bottleneck: check it before raising outreach volume. Count onboarding and offboarding hours per client: if they grow while tenure falls, check fit (F12 red flags) before adding outreach. Build extra delivery capacity a quarter before a planned push, not during it.
- D12 [running the core offer's delivery] [shape: service] Deliver the first emotional win in week one (O26), show it on the progress tracker (O23), and point out every short-term benefit they notice. Set short task deadlines inside each week for several visible wins and specific praise per week; never let good work by client staff pass without a word. Each group hears consented, anonymised wins from other clients, with no client personal data (X5) and no fee or customer details shared between clients. Give each weekly task and recording a one-line reason tied to their goal, true only (O11). At the end of weeks 1 and 2, the named lead writes a few lines in the tracker, in their own words, on what has changed: no reward, no need to be positive, private unless later published under M45 with written consent; reuse it at the follow-on call (M8, M40), and compare later task completion with groups without it. Rewards for tasks stay small and about the result (specific praise, recognition of the named lead), never big outside prizes (see the Notes on M30 and M38). Keep missed-action fees as small as the legitimate interest allows (X3), never the main reason tasks get done. Give the weekly reason as the team's own goal first; the guarantee conditions are stated clearly at kickoff and in the terms (O8), not used as the weekly push. Never rank clients or staff against each other: show progress against the client's own day-1 baseline, and suggest one weekly team number posted where staff see it beside last week's, with no comment. Group updates report how many have done a step, never who is behind; laggards are chased one to one (D13). Wherever a client sees itself against an average (L32), an above-average figure carries one plain line of approval, and published averages also say what the top clients did. Open each tracker with the steps genuinely done already ticked, never filler. Give each group one shared, true goal tied to a real date and celebrate the joint result. Deliver good news personally and fast, let the tracker carry weak numbers with the next step beside them, and name the part of the method in every win message. On the day a client's first workflow goes live, send its named staff a short personal note naming what they did (a card or message at most, M27). At milestone reviews, thank the named lead in front of their boss, with their agreement. In the client's busiest weeks, send only short, warm messages with no new tasks or asks, and hold reviews until the week after. The last session is a results round: each client states its day-1 and end counts (D8), recorded only with consent, with no client personal data (X5) and no fees or customer details (M12); it gives proof and leads into the follow-on call (M8). Show the named lead's D7 goal at the top of the tracker and reread it midway; after the opening report (D14), give one true short story tied to the week's task. Test after 3 paid clients: the named lead picks, from 3 true options, a plain label for what the team now does, never implying accreditation (O25; see X9), at most a desk card the same for everyone, judged on M9 retention by group.
- D13 [training a client's staff] [shape: service] Give fast, in-the-moment correction of the step, not the person: name the gap between what happened and the checklist step, in the D16 order, using stop, start, keep, and teach as "under this condition, do this". When a team has not done a task, assume good intent, say it lightly and confidently, then give the next step; never blame. Never agree with a staff member's "not techy" label: treat it as a missing step, give one small rep they can finish in the session, and praise it. In group sessions, praise specific actions in front of the group (naming people only with their agreement) and correct one to one in private, with the checklist step and an example. Once clients consent, record staff from more than one client (not the owner) running a workflow (X5) and show doubtful staff short clips several times in week 1: a peer model works better than the owner, and several beat one. Ask each client's named lead by name for their one action or question, never "any questions?" to the room, and give each client a private question route. Every named staff member gets one small part to say or do in each session; never skip the quiet or least confident person. When one staff member dismisses another, point to the shared deadline and show the team how to draw the quieter colleague out. Greet and thank by name the junior staff who do the work. When a client's draft misses, name what works, give 2 or 3 lines to redraft along, and edit their second version with them. When a workflow asks staff to drop a habit they are proud of, say what is good in it, say plainly it is a real change, tie it to their goal, leave it until the next session and praise the switch. When a client slips, cite its own best week as the standard, give the gap in its numbers and offer to find the fix together. When a client owns a miss in a group session, thank them, say misses are normal in a new workflow's first weeks and state confidence in the next step; the detail goes one to one. Introduce each new tool step as a skill staff already use. Suggest to the named lead that the most sceptical or fussiest staff member owns the step their trait suits, with a true reason; if a named lead is not working out, suggest privately to their boss a co-owner or a narrower role, never in a group; the client decides its roles. Open each session by asking a named person for the last step they got working (a count, D8); when someone doubts they can run a workflow, point to a true past change they handled and a step they have already done. Before any hard conversation (tasks not done, an upset client, a refund or cancellation, asking a pilot for full price, F4), open with the goal the client set in its own words at kickoff (D7), then the issue and the next step; true goals only, never a manufactured "we" (O11). For a doubtful long-serving staff member, show a clip of a similarly long-serving, once-sceptical person (consent, X5). On how a workflow is actually run, ask the person who does the work before their boss. Ask everyone to set their display name to first name and organisation. Take anything ambiguous or emotionally loaded by phone or video first, then a short written recap. In session one, say how corrections will come (the checklist step, one at a time, in private, D16) and how to use one: note the step, redo it, ask if unclear; a correction is about the step, never about them, and they should say if one landed badly. Start each workflow with the named lead plus the most respected staff member, let colleagues see it working on the first batch, then widen.
- D14 [any meeting, session or client conversation, or someone raises a problem] Communication that changes nobody's behaviour wastes both sides' time. Before or at the start, ask "what do you want to happen as a result of this?" and "what should we start or stop doing?". When someone raises a problem, let them finish and acknowledge it first if raised with feeling, then ask what change in behaviour would stop it happening again. If someone rejects something mid-session, thank them, say the owner will think it over and mean it, book the follow-up for the same or next working day and run the F27 questions first; a compliance error is still stated plainly on the spot (D16). If the owner does not understand what someone means, ask plainly, and seek clarity rather than agreement. Reduce any task or role to the few actions that drive results, then ask how to do more of them, faster or better. No standing status calls with a client: every session ends with changed actions, and ad hoc 15-minute calls are booked only to solve a named problem. Record each action as action, owner, hours: what it is, which named person owns it, how many hours it takes (ask for hours, not a day) and when it is due. End every session by recapping the actions in order, rereading the full list each time one is added, and ask each owner to say back what they will do and by when; then each owner types their own actions and dates into the shared tracker in their own words, and in group sessions says their next action to the group, only with their agreement (nothing on fees, customer lists or customer details, M12). When someone has competing work, ask how many hours each task takes and set the order: prioritising is the lead's job. When a team says it lacks hours for the tasks, explain what that means for its result, then ask how it could fit (swap a task, move a slot, who covers) before proposing a fix, and record the answer as action, owner, hours. Open every session with each named lead reporting, in their own words, what they did since last time and the result as a count; log it under D8 and, with consent, in the C14 story bank and L32 proof. When the owner raises a problem in a client's workflow, give the next step and owner in the same message; if the plan is not ready, raise it anyway and say when the plan will follow (a compliance error is never delayed, D16). In any session over about 60 minutes, recap the key points and actions at the midpoint as well as the end. Write each session as a timed plan (segments in minutes, each ending in an action), and after each session cut or shorten one segment that produced no action (D15). Test (L63) in pilot and milestone reviews, never sales calls (C5): open with a one-page written summary read silently for 3 to 5 minutes, then ask each person's first reaction, the most junior first and the most senior last, and compare actions agreed against reviews without it.
- D15 [once a fortnight while delivering] [shape: service] Compare one recorded sales call with that client's first session: was every promise delivered, what easy extras did they expect, and what should change in the pitch or the delivery (O29, M13, L65)? Once a quarter, check that every buyer-facing step (cold message, lead magnet headline, video opener, call agenda, kickoff, booking page, offer document, agreement message, payment page) uses the same promise, the same words and the same frame (L11), with the guarantee's name and the owner's photo at each decision point. Every 10 sessions, compare those with the most actions done by the next session against those with the fewest: do more of what the top ones share and less of what the bottom ones share. Log hours spent on the core delivery materials, and before each intake review every changed delivery asset on at least 3 separate days before using it (delivery assets only: outreach copy stays send before tweaking, L16, L50). Judge the core materials on logged hours (hundreds over the year, not tens, within F1 hours), with selling still first (F3, F29). Before the first pilot, write a one-page service standard (replies within the stated time, a recap within 24 hours, materials before each session, sessions on time, client data under X5), every stated time true (O8; see X9), and score delivery against it at each fortnightly review, treating a miss as a slip under L75. Two weeks before each kickoff and before any new workflow goes live, run a written go/no-go check (the owner can run it from the checklist alone, D5; small batch tested, D3; data processing agreement signed, X5; manual fallback written, D3) and fix any red item in that fortnight, never planning on a regulator or vendor slipping a date. Before pilots, run a made-up test client through a full cycle of the work in the common tools' free trials, logging where it hurts, with no real client data (X5), and before each intake try any new rule, software or tool change that touches the workflows on it first. Once there are paying clients, review weekly instead of fortnightly while any refund or complaint has arrived in the last 30 days.
- D16 [giving feedback or correcting anyone: client staff, their bosses, suppliers, later the owner's team] Correction order. A compliance error is stated plainly and promptly first (X4); the rest is about how. Begin with true, specific praise naming the exact action and why it matters to their result. Join praise and correction with "and", not "but". Mention a true slip of the owner's own where there is one. Ask questions rather than give orders: when a step is skipped, first ask whether it is awkward or does not fit their tools, then give its reason in their terms, then the next step; let them finish, and log the reason as a checklist fix (D5). Checklist steps stay exact; requests and changes go as questions, and nobody is left to learn from a slip that reaches the client's customers. Correct in private. Hints and questions suit senior or sensitive people; junior staff get the step said plainly and kindly, because a missed hint is not feedback (D5). Make the fault seem easy to fix with one small next step. Then say the one result needed and the support the owner will give; the same order works for a stalled pilot (F4) or a client going quiet (F12). Praise even the slightest real improvement, every time, specific and sincere. Give them a true reputation to live up to. When asking a client to change something, open with one true line of thanks, say what the current way costs them, ask "when possible", and end with their gain. Never invent the praise, the slip or the reputation (O11, M27).
- D17 [any login, account, payment detail or data the owner holds: leads, pilots, clients] Security basics, now (see X8). Multi-factor login and a password manager on every business account (email, domain, bank, payment provider, lead sheet, AI tools, booking tool); open every business account on the business email, never a personal one; give any helper their own least-access login (T2) and keep a written list of who can reach money, client data and the lead sheet; back up the lead sheet and client records weekly to a second place the owner controls (L36); before paying any changed bank details, confirm by phone on a number already held; keep software and devices updated; encrypt laptops, phones and any exported client or lead files. Aim for the project's country's baseline security certification (see X8). Number: accounts with multi-factor on, out of total, target 100%.
