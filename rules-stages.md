## Scaling roadmap (S)

- S0 [at roadmap stage 0 (see F32), asking what to do next, planning the week, or reaching this stage's exit] Improvise (solo, giving it away free). Headcount 1, the owner alone; the owner's role is researcher, gathering information and feedback. Main problem: nothing is happening yet, and the owner's personal money, business money and personal risk are all the same thing. Exit: people use the offer free and F10's exit for this stage is met; where the two differ, the stricter one wins (F10, M5, D8); the gate counts deferred and N/A steps as closed. Product: run the free pilots M5 sets inside the avatar, each with written commitments, and measure the result at set points (M5, F12, D8). Marketing: offer the lead magnet as the free entry that reveals the next problem the core offer solves (O14, M29). Sales: reach prospects only through the one channel chosen under L4 (L4, L7, L10, L52). Customer success: capture each pilot's proof at set milestones, with the client's written consent (L32, D8, L33; see X13). IT and security: keep every business account, the sending domain and the lead list in the owner's own name and under the owner's control (L6, L8, L36). Recruiting: no recruiting at this stage. HR: if the owner is employed elsewhere, check that contract's outside-interests terms before going public or invoicing anyone (F1). Decide the legal form of the business with a qualified adviser, after that check and before the first invoice, weighing a company's liability shield against its filing duties (F1, T3; see X20). Finance and legal: open a separate business account, fund it and pay every business cost from it, starting now while it is easy (M26, M19). Carry two cards, one business and one personal, and ask which one it is at every payment (M19, F2). Keep every receipt from before trading starts and claim only the business share of dual-use costs, as a qualified adviser advises (M19; see X19). Expect to owe tax on profit and hold M19's reserve as each payment lands (M19; see X19). If the business takes a credit card, clear it in full every month and never borrow to fund outreach (M19, M26). Get insurance quotes for professional indemnity and cyber cover, plus public liability if any work happens on a client's premises, before the first pilot starts (O42, L75, D7). Check whether a data protection fee is due once the lead list holds personal data (X2, L8; see X8). Fix spending habits before chasing clients if they are not under control (M19, F2).

- S1 [at roadmap stage 1 (see F32), asking what to do next, planning the week, or reaching this stage's exit] Monetize (solo, first paid sale). Headcount 1, the owner alone; the owner's role shifts from researcher to starter, and by the end of this stage the business is officially a business. Main problem: the business makes no money, because the core offer is not yet good enough to charge for, no one knows the owner sells anything, the owner does not yet know how to sell, and there is no way to collect money. Exit: the first full-price sale whose delivery cost, the owner's hours at M1's rate included, clears M1's margin bar, with the margin logged against the sale (F10, M1); the gate counts deferred and N/A steps as closed. Product: fix the core offer from what the pilots show until it is good enough to charge for, and release it as V1 (O30, M5, L75). List every non-monetary hidden cost that stops a prospect buying, including the ones outside the delivery itself (O28, L34). Ask every prospect who says no what makes it a no, what would make it a yes and what it would take, then price the answer (F5, L34, C14). Test extreme hypotheticals on paper to find the biggest friction, and decide whether to do one only once a price is attached (O43, O31, F5). Work backwards from the client's ideal state to what the business can deliver, making yes as easy as possible (O30, O7, O21). Price above most of the market so the delivery can be different and more valuable (M2, M4, M7). Do the unscalable work the first clients need, and systemise it only once there is money (M22, F30). Score the core offer on the value equation and cut time delay and effort first (O21, O22, O28). Marketing: tell prospects the business now sells a paid offer, using pilot results as the proof (L32, M5, L39). Show proof that is specific, recent, numbered, from a similar client, shown rather than told, confirmed by someone other than the owner, and plenty of it (L32, M45; see X13). Have the answer to who else the business has worked with ready before anyone asks (C9, L32). Sales: use the pilot results to offer paid places, closing on a call and offering to solve their problem for money (C1, C5, L52). Raise to the full price as M5 sets out and report it with the stage line (M5, F10). If prospects will not buy, check the proof, whether the owner actually asked, whether the script was followed and whether the owner asked with conviction (L34, L42, C13). Customer success: talk to every pilot client, ignore scalability for now, and list every problem they show (D9, M22). Note for each problem whether it is about the result or the experience (D9, M9). Try visibly hard, because a new business wins on effort before it wins on polish (O11, M22). Fix results by setting expectations in the sale: how and how often the owner communicates, reply times, hours, their goals and the milestones between (D7, L65, O26). Fix experience by making it easier, faster and more pleasant (O22, O7). IT and security: pick only the free or trial tools the work needs, each approved by hand (F2, F16, F15). Recruiting: fill a skill or time gap with a freelancer paid for output, never an employee (T2, T3). HR: before any supplier starts, agree a one-page written agreement covering who is involved, who does what, where, by when, how much, for how long, who signs off and what happens if it is late or not good (T2, X3). Finance and legal: set up at least two ways to get paid, a recurring payment method plus a backup (C1, M42, L36). Before the first paid sale, run a live payment end to end and see it land in the business account (C1, L36).

- S2 [at roadmap stage 2 (see F32), asking what to do next, planning the week, or reaching this stage's exit] Advertise (solo, consistent leads). Headcount 1, the owner alone; the owner's role is doer, in the weeds, leading only freelancers, and the owner copies what founders did at this stage rather than what they do now (F28, T3). Main problem: new clients arrive inconsistently because outreach stops and starts, paying clients expect more than pilots did, conversions swing, paid clients complain more, and money is not yet tracked or tax set aside properly. Exit: the first target (F3) is met, and either the first hire is made or, where the owner has decided not to hire yet (T3), D11 shows the owner's hours binding within a quarter (F3, F10, D11, T3); the gate counts deferred and N/A steps as closed. Product: make sure every paying client gets the result, doing extra unscalable work inside the productised steps and costing it into the price (M22, M13, O6). Never trade valuable for scalable at this stage (F30, M22). Marketing: make a set number of new first touches on the channel every working day, follow-ups first, not only when clients are needed (L41, L6, L70). Keep outreach going when delivery is full, and raise the price rather than stop (L70, M5, O35). Stay on the one channel (L4, L40). Use content as support only; content as a channel waits until after the first target (L4, L58, L59, L60). Sales: nail one script for booking and closing, with a pitch of no more than 3 minutes on three pillars, and leave it unchanged for 100 conversations (C5, L16, C13). Spend most of the call on them, running the pain cycle until nothing is left (C5, C2). Keep two or three rehearsed answers for each concern type: time, stall, money, preferences and authority (C14). Reinforce the decision and hand over straight into onboarding (C5, D7). Keep the booking call to clarifying and qualifying, as C3 sets out; a separate setter waits until hiring starts (C3, T1, T3). Keep the conversation flowing by acknowledging what they said, linking it to the best clients, then asking the next question (C14, L13). Customer success: handle every upset client in the HEAR order (hear them out, empathise, apologise for the impact, resolve with them) and never back down or shrug (L75, M27, D16). Resolve inside M27's order and L75's one make-good, the same for every client (M27, L75). Capture a testimonial from each happy client with M45's success interview: the struggle, the numbers before, the scepticism, why this business and why then, the numbers after, and what changed for them (M45, L37; see X13). IT and security: use the lead list as the CRM and pipeline, with a simple contact route and booking link (L8, L44, L30, F29). Automate auto-replies and booking reminders, mark automated messages as automated, and offer two set times rather than an open question (C3, D10). Recruiting: turn the best freelancer into an employee and post job adverts once hiring starts; until then both wait (T1, T3). HR: check any regular freelancer's tax and employment status before relying on them (T2; see X18, X19). Putting anyone on payroll waits until hiring starts (T1, T3). Finance and legal: keep the books in accounting software and check the business account daily (M26, D10). Save for tax as each payment lands (M19; see X19). Have a qualified adviser set the reserve rates, and skip any write-off that sounds risky (M19).

- S3 [at roadmap stage 3 (see F32), asking what to do next, planning the week, or reaching this stage's exit] Stabilize (1 to 4 employees). Headcount 1 to 4 employees; the owner's role is trainer. Main problem: workload exceeds current resources, and volume creates cracks the owner has no time to fix. Exit: the first people are hired; the gate counts deferred and N/A steps as closed. F10's later-stage unlocks (premium tier, upsells, done-for-you, partners) apply from S3 entry (F10, M16, M18, O38). Product: fix the top complaint or the biggest activation blocker (D9); force-rank fixes on effect, likelihood and investment (L63); define activation by the outcome plus the actions that lead to it (O26); name one big hairy problem with a money tag (F28). Marketing: long-term nurture email to opted-in contacts (L57, X2; see X2); content on one platform as nurture (L58). Sales: basic qualifications drawn from the worst clients, using budget, authority, need and timing (C2, F12); appointment setting and calendaring (C3). Customer success: one ordered onboarding sequence to activation (D7); the next meeting booked at every meeting (C11); a defined support route and reply times (D7, L13). IT and security: cancel unused tools (F2); stretch the free trial of each tool kept with credits or discounts where offered, each still approved by hand (F2); basic pipeline stages including back-outs (L30). Recruiting: job description written as an advert (T1); CV review against evidence; if far fewer applications pass to a screening call than expected, fix the advert or the CV review, setting the expected pass rate from the business's own data (T1); hire for this stage and the next 12 to 18 months only (T1). HR: register as an employer and set up payroll, pensions and the compulsory insurance before the first employee starts (T1, O42; see X18). Finance and legal: payroll, keeping the books, money owed in and money owed out (M19); suppliers paid on time (T2); every client agreement entered in billing and failed payments chased (C1); fixed bills reserved first (M19); one inbox for incoming bills (D10); give collections an owner early, ahead of other hires (C1, M26). Council at stage entry on nurture cadence (L57). Council at stage entry on job descriptions and the hiring funnel (T1), weighing the screening pass rate. Council at stage entry on workforce planning and first hires (T3, F28, M22, D11, F11), weighing collections as an early hire.

- S4 [at roadmap stage 4 (see F32), asking what to do next, planning the week, or reaching this stage's exit] Prioritize (5 to 9 employees). Headcount 5 to 9; the owner's role is manager, with one layer, a full first team. Main problem: trying to be everything to everyone. Exit: niche down to serve only clients like the best clients; the gate counts deferred and N/A steps as closed. Product: 80/20 the client base and replicate the best 20% (F12); specialise product, avatar and price together as V2 (L2, M2); price to the avatar the business serves best, not the weakest client it can barely get results for, since a narrower target raises perceived value even when the deliverable stays the same (M2, L2). Marketing: better free value gated behind qualifications (O14, L44); free value and callout matched to the narrower avatar (O12, O15); more creative volume (O13); niche qualifications as friction (L15). Sales: weekly KPIs by rep in a spreadsheet (L30); speed to contact (L72); a thank-you page that sells the booking, with scarcity only if true (O36; see X10); scheduler above the fold, 15-minute slots within the owner's set hours (C3); automated plus manual reminders (C3); pay setters about 3% of each sale they set that closes, on top of their legal minimum pay, and have them send the manual reminders (C3, T1; see X18); praise leading indicators, show rate first (L30). Customer success: CS KPIs and one set of notes per client in a basic CRM (M9); track NPS, CSAT, monthly churn and refund and chargeback rates; use shallow feedback first (NPS, CES, CSAT), then open-text surveys, then interviews and focus groups; ask CSAT straight after a value point (support, onboarding, help materials); score NPS with 9 or 10 as promoters, 7 or 8 as neutral and 0 to 6 as detractors, on a scale of minus 100 to plus 100, quoting no benchmark figures (M9); 5 minutes' prep before every client call (C4); revenue churn tracked alongside client churn (M9). IT and security: everyone on the shared tools: password manager, team chat, and one project tool used daily (T2). Recruiting: short screening call, then one interview for culture, skill and potential, then references (T1). HR: a written statement of each employee's terms by day one, separate from the handbook, and an employee handbook with disciplinary and grievance procedures, working time, leave and equal treatment sections (T1; see X18); add to the handbook a code of conduct (conflict of interest, confidentiality, drugs, alcohol, relationships at work), work schedules, attendance and remote or office working, pay, overtime and bonus rules, how performance is judged, health and safety, technology use and company equipment including its return on leaving, the resignation and termination process with notice and exit interviews, and a signed acknowledgement of receipt, with confidentiality and technology use tied to X5 (T1, X5); write a health and safety policy once the headcount requires one (T1, O42; see X18). Finance and legal: P&L and cash flow statement (M1, M26); public liability and employers' liability insurance (O42; see X18); plan for lumpy recurring costs (M19). Council at stage entry on client health and churn economics (M9, M42), weighing the CS KPI set, feedback depths, CSAT timing and NPS scoring. Council at stage entry on interviews, panels, scoring and references (T1). Council at stage entry on financial reporting and the finance team (M1, M26, F11, M23).

- S5 [at roadmap stage 5 (see F32), asking what to do next, planning the week, or reaching this stage's exit] Productize (10 to 19 employees). Headcount 10 to 19; the owner's role is director, with two or three small managers. Main problem: nothing else to sell existing clients, so lifetime value is too low against acquisition cost. Exit: make and sell a second premium product to existing clients while reinforcing the infrastructure; the gate counts deferred and N/A steps as closed. Product: back-end upsell at about 5x the core (M16); eight ways to raise client value (M31, M32, M39); connect customer success with product, so what CS hears shapes the second product (M16, D9). Marketing: more spend on niched messaging for the new offer (O12); a higher acquisition-cost limit once the second product counts (M23); the business scores leads (L8); the split-test order is parked for the paid growth council below, with O18 standing until then (L45, O18, L63); attribution software (L14); 30-day cash collected (M20). Sales: sales aids and a pre-call video (C2); weekly cross-team game tape (D15, D14); NPS, CSAT and refunds by rep (M9); state the facts and tell the truth (M27, O47; see X9). Customer success: before hiring more support staff, sort the questions into 3 to 5 categories and write a template answer for each (D6, L59, D11); a written list of what reps may decide alone (T1); the ascension path is parked for the upsells council below, with M31 standing until then (M31, M36). IT and security: company-owned devices and a dashboard (F16, L30); buy staff good devices, since poor kit costs output and pushes staff onto personal devices, and insure the equipment (F16, O42); a device return policy, with any pay deduction agreed in writing first (T1; see X18). Recruiting: internal candidates through the normal process or a 60 to 90 day trial (T1); a staff referral bonus set against agency cost, paid through payroll, with its terms, such as payment once the hire is productive at day 90, in the contract or referral policy (T1; see X18, X19). HR: a termination policy with a fair process, formal written warnings and a reasonable improvement period (T1, D16; see X18); coverage planned before any exit; final pay with accrued holiday and notice (see X18); inside that process, use the traffic-light talk (red, yellow or green, and what gets them back to green) and name the reason (culture fit, skill fit or will), time any exit away from a big sales day, at the end of the day for sales staff and just before a team meeting, with two hours blocked for the team's questions, then follow a scripted conversation and a written loose-ends email covering final pay, benefits and equipment return (T1, D16); make new kit part of a new starter's welcome, and frame each department head as a company leader who happens to run a function (T1). Finance and legal: a savings plan for big expenses tied to capacity triggers (M19, D11); a cash projection 6 to 12 months ahead on the current run rate (M26, F11); allowable expenses and tax allowances tracked through the year (M19; see X19). Council at stage entry on client health and churn economics (M9, M42). Council at stage entry on upsells and ascension (M31, M8, M36, M16), weighing five ascension points (straight after purchase; 24 to 48 hours later, after onboarding or first value; the first big win; halfway; a last shot), each with a leading indicator, a different ask and a fitting incentive, and real effort in every ask, against M31's no pitch straight after a win, with the early-bird discount still barred by M36; and connecting CS with product. Council at stage entry on lead scoring and routing (L8, L54). Council at stage entry on paid growth: ads, brand, attribution and payback (L61, L73, L18, M20, M23, L60, L76), weighing a split-test order of headline and subheadline first, then offer, image, benefit bullets, proof and page order, against O18's offer first; accepting several months' loss per client when the numbers are known, against M20; and ad platform credits for new advertisers, deferred under L4 and L61. Council at stage entry on game tape and coaching (D15, T1). Council at stage entry on the support playbook and limits (L59, D6, T1, M2, M36). Council at stage entry on sourcing and senior talent (T1, T2). Council at stage entry on performance, culture and firing (T1, D16), weighing the traffic-light talk and exit timing. Council at stage entry on workforce planning and first hires (T3, F28, M22, D11, F11). Council at stage entry on team tools and systems (M19, T2, F16, D5, F29), weighing staff device quality, equipment insurance and kit as part of the welcome. Council at stage entry on financial reporting and the finance team (M1, M26, F11, M23).

- S6 [at roadmap stage 6 (see F32), asking what to do next, planning the week, or reaching this stage's exit] Optimize (20 to 49 employees). Headcount 20 to 49; the owner's role is leader, with two full layers of managers below. Main problem: time and resources wasted on a growing workload, with more of everything. Exit: grow by doing everything better, not by doing more; the gate counts deferred and N/A steps as closed. Product: one improvement process for both products: list, score, rank, fix one at a time (L63, F30); prune with the keep-one question (D9); members told before anything is removed (M40, M22); do a prerequisite fix first, and accept the business staying imperfect, since perfecting every function at once makes everything mediocre (F22, L63). Marketing: weekly ad assembly, many hooks, few bodies, few calls to action (L73, L61, O17); theme ad bodies on the service, people who have done it, teaching or a story, and end each ad by showing the next page and the click-to-submit step, so the page matches what they expect (L61, L73). Sales: a training system of best calls, script and tone guide (C13); a coaching cadence for new reps (T1); sales game tape with the rep's self-review first (D15). Customer success: cohorts by sign-up month and product (M9); stay incentive timing is parked for the client health council below, with M42 standing until then (M42); cancelling kept easy (X3); activation mapped, tested one change at a time, then built into the offer (O26, D12). IT and security: company email for every account, password manager, onboarding and offboarding checklists (T2); before offboarding, check which accounts are registered to each company email address, to catch any outside the password manager (D17); role-based security training; bank-detail changes confirmed by phone on a known number; five protocols (firewall, encryption, multi-factor login, patching, and access control with a list of who can reach high-risk systems) (X5, D17; see X20). Recruiting: headhunt senior roles (T1); four interviewers for the four fits, senior roles only; references by seniority; for manager and director roles, take four or five references of 30 to 45 minutes each, with the same questions to every referee and two follow-ups on every answer (T1); below about 50 staff, the owner does the final interview and approves the offer and package for every management hire (T1); treat pay negotiation by senior candidates as normal, expect several pay calls for director roles, and talk about money openly from the start (T1). HR: meet the legal floor for pensions and benefits (T1; see X18, X19); advanced pay plans only when hiring or keeping people fails; choose benefits by what the team values, not what the owner values: find the workforce's needs (age mix, dependants) and the legal duties, pick a provider on industry fit, reputation and an easy portal, have it cost each plan for an individual contributor, a manager and a director, and pick one that handles the admin; launch benefits as something to sell, collect feedback quarterly, re-price at each annual renewal, and use staff's reasons for staying as the recruiting pitch (T1); for advanced pay, choose the goal first (attract, retain or both), treat base, bonus and benefits as one package, lead with performance pay at this size, benchmark against the market first and add no complexity if already at benchmark, tie career steps to pay steps, and keep one pay template per management level, reviewed yearly (T1). Finance and legal: gross margin and lifetime value by product (M1); a balance sheet including IP, trademarks and domains, with a trademark check (see X20); money owed in and out staffed, collections as a conversation (M27); core suppliers paid on time (T2); department cost against return (M1); tax set aside (M19; see X19); cyber and business interruption insurance (O42). Council at stage entry on client health and churn economics (M9, M42), weighing a stay incentive one to two months before the usual churn point, since people decide to leave early, reading the four to eight weeks before churn, against M42's discount just past it. Council at stage entry on pruning and customer-led product (D9, M40, O29, M4, O2). Council at stage entry on paid growth: ads, brand, attribution and payback (L61, L73, L18, M20, M23, L60, L76), weighing ad-body themes and showing the next page in each ad. Council at stage entry on game tape and coaching (D15, T1). Council at stage entry on interviews, panels, scoring and references (T1), weighing senior reference depth and the owner's part in management hires. Council at stage entry on sourcing and senior talent (T1, T2). Council at stage entry on pay design (T1), weighing benefits selection and the advanced pay steps. Council at stage entry on financial reporting and the finance team (M1, M26, F11, M23). Council at stage entry on portfolio bets and exit readiness (F30, M10, F10).

Headcount 50 to 99; the owner's role is executive, with managers, directors and executives above individual contributors. Main problem: everything is disorganised. Exit: triage and sort everything into buckets, passing 100 employees; the gate counts deferred and N/A steps as closed. Product: one problem, one team, one measurable objective, biggest payoff first (F30, L63). Marketing: a second channel funded by referrals and segmented nurture (L62, L65, L66); the lowest-risk channel step first (L62); run by a zero-to-one builder, with the owner close to it; judge it over months, not 30 days; channel one's best person may move to it only after training a replacement who hits the same numbers; never put a new hire on a new channel, and never give one person two initiatives (F10). Sales: wider booking coverage and a nurture checklist (C3); weekend and evening shifts within working time law (see X18); caller ID shows a genuine number (see X6); show rate praised as much as close rate (L30); pay setters about 3% of sales, introduce lead, setter and closer in one three-way message, and let the month's top closer pick their schedule first, checked against L54's commission on cash collected (L54, T1). Customer success: a proactive four-R journey map built into the CRM, with time, behaviour and event triggers (L65, L66, M45); retain and review before referrals and resales; simpler offers (M13); once the proactive tasks are mapped, split customer service into reactive and proactive teams and hire for the proactive side (T1); read complaints and negative feedback for add-on, upsell or new product ideas (D9). IT and security: a central cloud host with several backups (L36); a central hardware register; naming and folder rules; a map of where personal data sits (X5; see X8). Recruiting: an applicant tracking system chosen for ease of use; specialist recruiters picked on placement rate and speed, one trial role first (T2); choose an applicant tracking system and HR system that integrate with payroll, accounting software, the CRM and each other (T2); use applicant tracking automation (CV parsing, interview scheduling with open slots offered, candidate matching) so recruiters stop spending their time on CVs, keeping a human review of any automated rejection (T1; see X8); list the roles hired most and use specialist recruiters only for job-specific roles (sales, support, IT), keeping roles specific to the business in-house (T1, T2). HR: an HR system covering payroll, statutory holiday and sick pay, performance management first (see X18); systems sized for the next 18 to 36 months; stakeholders test demos, trial before a contract, never a vendor's biggest customer (F2); never take a software vendor's first offer: negotiate the rate, start date, escalation clauses and fixed annual increases before signing a yearly contract (T2, M19); make the culture deliberate: measure what already produces good candidates and good performance, find the actions behind it, and keep them as headcount grows (T1). Finance and legal: a quarterly expense review (F2); department budgets set after watching actual spend; a spending SOP with card limits; choose a business card programme that sets and changes each person's limit separately, not the bank's default, with rewards matched to the biggest spend categories (M19, F2); use department budgets to give leaders autonomy as well as to cap spend, coach under-spenders to judge a purchase by what not having it costs in hours against the price, and expect senior hires to size their solutions to the budget (M1, F2); 12-month operating projections; more insurance as exposure grows (O42). Council at stage entry on nurture cadence (L57). Council at stage entry on the post-sale journey (L65, M31, D8, F29, M32). Council at stage entry on lead scoring and routing (L8, L54). Council at stage entry on the second channel (L62, F3, F10, F22), weighing who runs the new channel. Council at stage entry on the support playbook and limits (L59, D6, T1, M2, M36), weighing the reactive and proactive support split. - S8 [at roadmap stage 8 (see F32), asking what to do next, planning the week, or reaching this stage's exit] Specialize (100 to 250 employees). Headcount 100 to 250; the owner's role is president, with a full executive team and about four layers, added at the bottom. Main problem: no one is good enough to know everything, and the owner's focus is too broad. Exit: dedicated people and teams for specific tasks; the gate counts deferred and N/A steps as closed. Product: survey, prune and improve by deletion (D9, M40); a value against willingness-to-pay grid (O32); pruned items sold as add-ons to those who value them (M11). Marketing: standardised messaging around one to three words (O3); big free value for the avatar (L46, O19); brand associations that fit now (O2). Sales: automated metrics by platform, rep and product (L14, L30); lead assignment by lead type (L54); front-end and back-end specialists. Customer success: a blended journey with product paths in parallel (M31, M32); a separate front end and onboarding for direct premium buyers (M7, M33); tag every client in the CRM with their product path, and give each path its own onboarding, email sequence and support segment, automated so no client is onboarded twice (M31, M32, F29); after mapping every path, cut the journeys or segments with the lowest lifetime value and most team effort and move that capacity to the best paths, solving for the highest lifetime value and lowest operational complexity (M31, M32, D9); test product order on lifetime value at equal acquisition cost: A then B, B then A, and both together (M31, L63).
