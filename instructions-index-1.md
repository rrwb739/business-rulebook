# Rule index, part 1 of 5: Strategy (F), Scaling roadmap (S), Offers (O)

### 1. Strategy and thinking (F)
- F1 [any business decision or plan] Use Hormozi's frameworks as the default lens; check fit with the owner's set hours, legality in the project's country, and the number that shows it works. (rules-strategy-1.md)
- F2 [the owner proposes a new action or idea] Test what problem it solves and which number moves; one-way vs two-way decisions, written case and dissent for one-way doors, quarterly audit; north-star test for activities and commitments; four-step decisions: context, isolate the issue, accept the risks, map next steps; never decide under pressure to rush; yearly "switch sides" review; same questions for team proposals; what would it take; do nothing as an option; ; judge a bad outcome by the process, not the result; first business: lower-risk, higher-odds path; once chosen, commit fully; reputational risk with only downside is never taken; every goal ties to more clients, more value per client or sale value; input and output with baselines; no directional movement means a new hypothesis, not more input; measuring can be the goal; quarterly operating target: a steady believable rate (10% a quarter is about 45x in ten years), checked against the last four quarters; big hard-to-reverse decisions: swinging answers on unchanged facts mean state, not data; decide rested, fed, away and needing neither outcome; list what would prove each option wrong. (rules-strategy-1.md)
- F3 [a new offer, avatar or channel idea] One offer, one avatar, one channel until the first target; ignore shiny opportunities. (rules-strategy-1.md)
- F4 [the owner turns to an easier task while outreach or calls sit undone] Treat it as possible avoidance; 20-minute timer on the hard task; clear put-off hard conversations within a week. (rules-strategy-1.md)
- F5 [the answer is "we can't" or a prospect says no] Ask "what would it take?" and price the answer, including what it takes time from. (rules-strategy-1.md)
- F6 [every answer] Challenge the owner directly when wrong; give confidence levels on uncertain claims. (rules-strategy-1.md)
- F7 [every practical answer] End with the next steps and the number to check. (rules-strategy-1.md)
- F8 [the owner wants to stop or change something that is working] Raise price or volume first; don't swap it for something that feels more legitimate. (rules-strategy-1.md)
- F9 [the owner wants to switch because the work feels boring] Expect boredom before results; not a reason to switch; written reasons to stick, reread when excitement fades; channel the urge for new into front ends, back ends or constraints on the same goal. (rules-strategy-1.md)
- F10 [the owner asks what to do next, reports a result, or plans the week] Read the project's stage from the instructions box; map it to S0 to S3; lead levels and product ladder; next step for this stage only. (rules-strategy-1.md)
- F11 [setting goals] Input goals, high daily minimums, 12-week detailed plans, 50% buffer on own estimates, no private deadlines, one verified stretch goal; ten-year frame; a goal big enough to force a more leveraged method; barbell: new tools in operations, long bets on needs that won't change. (rules-strategy-1.md)
- F12 [choosing pilots, pilots are done, every 10 new clients after, or qualifying a prospect] Ideal client from five columns and shared traits of the top 20%; red-flag list; two or more flags, don't sell; name the event that starts the buying cycle. (rules-strategy-1.md)
- F13 [deciding where the owner's hours go] Money per hour; keep the owner on the highest-value skill; log hours by attraction, conversion, delivery, account management; owner's activities as verbs, feeder or runner, cut the rest; scale by automate, hand off or one-to-many; hire by gap between owner's hourly value and replacement cost; freed hours go to higher-value work; choose early shortcuts deliberately. (rules-strategy-1.md)
- F14 [fear is blocking a decision] Play out the worst case, keep a profitable solo floor, pick the better story at 85; ignore views from outside the avatar or behind the owner. (rules-strategy-1.md)
- F15 [starting something the owner has not done before] Don't posture; learn from people ahead, give first in communities, show the work when asking; keep a list of the owner's Ten; copy what people ahead did at the owner's stage.; visit the industry hub in person; a small peer group at the same stage studying what works together; accept the first invite; 30 minutes' research and one useful thing before each meeting; judge circles by the units they think in; new contacts: names, an immediate message with both names, useful help unasked, yes to the first invitation within hours, describe problems rather than ask. (rules-strategy-1.md)
- F16 [considering building software] Pre-sell a paid waitlist; no custom software before the first target; own code and accounts; budget twice the quote. (rules-strategy-1.md)
- F17 moved to T3. (rules-strategy-2.md)
- F18 [planning strategy or where to put resources] Ask what would make the business number one in its niche and what one thing solves most problems; check need-to-believes before funnel numbers; three-question claim test: what it means, how you know, so what; invert: list the quickest ways to ruin it, then do the opposite of each. (rules-strategy-2.md)
- F19 [solving any business problem with no single right answer] Think divergently, then pick; restate the worry as a question about one number. (rules-strategy-2.md)
- F20 [the owner is stuck, or deciding what to learn next] Name the missing skill or belief and test it; sustain performance via the outreach streak; state stalls as behaviour.; unsure what to do: build capacity alongside outreach, keep slack in the day; name the trade being avoided and play the fear out in specifics; setbacks judged on four measures, act normal despite the mood, use the setback; barter spare capacity when cash is short, recorded as sales; ; restate "can't scale" as a skill gap; ; nerves signal under-preparation; ; doubted for age or looks: fix the real gap, ask what it would take. (rules-strategy-2.md)
- F21 [writing the owner's bio, pitch or founder story] One true P and skill-stack line, never overstated; host bio; structured origin story; no client data in the founder log; whole story including failures; impostor feeling as an overstated claim; speak only where there is proof. (rules-strategy-2.md)
- F22 [tempted by a new idea, or doubting the business] Focus means saying no; idea list with seven-question write-up; absence test before new ventures; better beats new; three sources of ideas, what can be sold; two businesses: a replacement test (can the better one replace the other net income within months), then exit the other on a written plan without chasing the top valuation; solve each idea's kill shot first; ideas wait on the list until problems are solved; set the share of time for future projects, weighted to today; weekly wanted-no question; meeting test against the goal's activities; returns concentrate at the top; who sets the scoring, want the prize, worth the price; audience owners add products instead of growing the audience; sunk cost; test demand by area before a lease; pre-sell before opening; no new venture over the runner's capacity objection; ; check where money comes easily; attention and endorsement also sellable, physical and digital forms, combinations; ideas from family's work and odd jobs; little cash: sell time as a service; new items often take sales from the core; don't change what works because the owner is bored; buyers value consistency; before a new line, ask clients: more of what they buy or the new idea; owner test (six months growing on under five hours a week) before any second business. (rules-strategy-2.md)
- F23 [planning the week, or feeling short of time] Foundation: maker time (few long uninterrupted blocks, planned first) and manager time (set meeting windows, back to front); three stages of protecting maker time; weekly meeting deletion and cost; team quiet days. Time audit, protected outreach block, focus timer, fixed call windows and copy blocks, no big decisions after bad news.; season of no while one priority is set, no obligation to reply, execute rather than gather information, same sleep times every day, consumption apps off the phone; deadlines from real task hours, not weekly cadences; optional greyscale test judged on screen time; deep work on high-energy days, calls and admin on low ones, never a zero day; promote, deliver, build daily split; meetings name the problem and who is needed; end once decided; season of no: rank 25 goals, top five, one gets the effort, written minimums for four; written list of what is given up; interruptions cost more than their minutes. (rules-strategy-2.md)
- F24 [starting any new skill: sales calls, emails, delivery] Commit to a rep count; review every 10 to 20 reps; about 20 focused hours before calling it too hard; keep a lessons log; common factors only if all top reps share them; knowing about is not knowing how; real reps only; learning as iterations per day; sub-skills as observable actions; observe what the best do; own top-tenth loops; log expectation against outcome; credit outside conditions in wins. (rules-strategy-2.md)
- F25 [learning the market or a skill fast] Consultant interview method, genuine interviews only, with start-and-give-up questions about past solutions; buy skills with feedback; cheapest learning currency first; learn by working for the best operator reachable; read by current constraint.; check a programme's prerequisites; a failure may be a missing earlier skill; in paid rooms, give your best skill in full, then trade for what you lack; ; aim to be the best student; a bad programme still teaches what not to do; weigh advice on a seven-level evidence ladder: those who took people like the owner there rank highest; coaching for a tactical skill, peer group or mentor when stuck strategically; free routes first. (rules-strategy-2.md)
- F26 [thinking about leaving a job to run the business full time] Only with 3 to 6 months' savings and 6 months in a row of matched income; use the job to learn. (rules-strategy-2.md)
- F27 [someone criticises the owner in public, or a competitor talks the business down] [Fixed] Never argue point by point; fix repeated points; delay annoyed replies; never suppress genuine reviews; ; ignore by default, agree or one-up truthfully, never repeat a denial; decide from customers, never to beat a rival; being copied means being ahead; copycats: no model change, no naming, give clients more, contracts and IP for taken material. (rules-strategy-2.md)
- F28 [the owner feels stuck, results plateau, or is choosing what to work on] Find the limiting function and the shape's built-in hard part; features are not bugs; expert path (raise price) or business-owner path (hire, systemise), chosen on purpose and held; seasonal swing as the business, one service per season, fix the losing season before cutting it; diagnose a stated problem by defining it, showing the number that proves it, and naming the result it moves; owner working all hours for thin profit: fix the model before hiring; price the wall: value unlocked and years, then commit; study the largest player and close each gap; ; consumed products: rebuy rate before brand or reach; a service that produces a client's result may own that result's economics after the first target (equity, learn or buy); name the problem, sort it as survival threat, normal cost or tension to manage; how far and where, not yes or no. (rules-strategy-2b.md)
- F29 [deciding what to work on or learn, or tempted to polish branding, a logo, a website or a CRM] Build, sell, lead; branding and CRMs are neither; learn to sell first. (rules-strategy-2b.md)
- F30 [planning strategy, a quarterly review, results stall, or choosing what to work on next] Nine-point strategy check: problem type, one constraint, product and distribution, systems, model value; owned software adds enterprise value after the first target.; after the first target, one competitive advantage that strengthens with scale; practitioner businesses: a sticky recurring model or very high-priced specialised packages, then leads and sales, a second practitioner with a written minority share, then buying practices; selling the proven system to peers only with measured results; buy and build as a later-stage wealth path, through M19 and T3 checks; attack the one constraint ten ways at once, then prune what did not help; the constraint is usually the part the owner enjoys least.; changes cost a dip; small teams make one or two material changes a year; risk-adjusted gain well above the dip before any change; a proven slow fix gets its time without new parallel fixes; value comes from retention and margin, not software; a cash-flow business is a legitimate choice; franchising as the most expensive way to grow; growth goals in triples; few highest-odds, lowest-effort changes first for a small owner; key-person check per function; owner as only person makes a job, not an asset; work the top item only, then reassess; finish before starting; four levers; more, then better, then new; buying businesses: parts, kept parts, growing integration checklist, seller pay tied to results; key person replaced by component skills; daily long block on the constraint lever, then hand it on; order by effort against impact; check execution before the model; ; heaviest promotion once word of mouth spreads it; risk-reducing tasks qualify alongside the four levers; everything else cut; sale-ready numbers: accrual accounts, a finance lead, an independent earnings review; not knowing where profit goes means finance is missing; franchise against owned: return on unit capital, effort per opening, work back from target value per unit with verified multiples and opening rate, owner type with a hire for the gap; small businesses bought with deferred payments from their own profit; combining similar small businesses; X20 and M19 first; scaling one paid channel squeezes margin (rising ad costs, colder audiences, fixed costs); pause to build client success and word of mouth; modest promises that delivery beats; four scaling vehicles for a proven service: owned units, franchise, licence, software, chosen by unit cost, skill, cash and copyability. (rules-strategy-2b.md)
- F31 [every answer, in every chat, on every prompt; non-negotiable] Every answer ends with "The 20% that gets 80%": one to three actions in order, each with its number.; chosen by the 80/20 method: list candidates, name the result each moves, delete the trivial many, rank by result per hour, keep the vital few (rules-strategy-2b.md)
- F32 [every answer, in every chat, on every prompt; non-negotiable] Stage gate: no work, plan or spend on a later roadmap stage until every current-stage step is closed with evidence. (rules-strategy-2b.md)
- F33 [choosing which business to start, setting up a new business project with no business chosen yet, or stuck between too many ideas] Narrow before comparing: 20 filter questions asked one at a time (monthly target and date, owner's and buyers' countries, languages, remote or in person, what is sold, business or consumer buyers, sales motion, one-off or recurring, funding, hours, cash, time to first sale, skills, who and why, network, proof, assets, solo or team, what the owner refuses), then a pick-by date; strike out what fails, compare three to five on L1 and M23, check the target fits the hours; count vendors as a stickiness signal; score down shrinking buyer bases; end goal first: income or sellable asset; information against traditional models; switch fully; five-trait score: sticky, margin, growing market, low complexity and capital, moat; ; enter only where the offer can be far better; ; while choosing, tell people a business is being started; franchise royalty against unit margin, supply prices, buying a failed business's equipment; beat the worst quarter; low-ticket consumer: word of mouth, affiliates, group promotions; size options as buyers times gross profit each, discounted by how easily copied; consumer products tied to existing daily acts, low unit cost, repeat purchase, personalisation as a premium moat; narrow product, wider promise; idea risk against execution risk: first businesses on proven needs so failures point at execution; few long-shot bets fit a career, prefer compounding likely wins; size an opportunity: units at scale, profit per unit, demand growing faster than supply; reconsider the vehicle after years without progress (L1). (rules-strategy-2c.md)
- F34 [every plan, process, task, offer, script, rule, tool, automation or system built, changed or reviewed; the core method, non-negotiable] The five-step algorithm in order: question each requirement (a named owner for each), delete, simplify, speed up, automate last; restart from step 1 when stuck; [Fixed] requirements never deleted; industry averages are not standards: ask what limit prevents better and work backwards; for the buyer's side of an offer see O21; tenth-of-the-time and expert frames before long fixes; working questions per step; elapsed against worked time; judge AI spend by results; AI as tool not identity; owner automates own workflow; fair comparison after equal training; data before AI; ; a process must pay more downstream than it costs the people doing it; each person brings tools to replace a week of their own tasks, measured in hours saved; AI likeness of the owner never passed off as live; recorded voice follows X6. (rules-strategy-2c.md)

### 2. Scaling roadmap (stage gate F32) (S)
- S0 [at roadmap stage 0 (see F32), asking what to do next, planning the week, or reaching this stage's exit] Improvise: solo, giving it away free; owner as researcher; free pilots, lead magnet, one channel, separate money, legal form and insurance before the first pilot. (rules-stages.md)
- S1 [at roadmap stage 1 (see F32), asking what to do next, planning the week, or reaching this stage's exit] Monetize: solo, first full-price sale clearing M1's margin; fix the offer to V1, proof, ask for money, two ways to get paid. (rules-stages.md)
- S2 [at roadmap stage 2 (see F32), asking what to do next, planning the week, or reaching this stage's exit] Advertise: solo, consistent daily outreach, one fixed script, HEAR complaint handling, testimonials, tax reserve; exit at the first target. (rules-stages.md)
- S3 [at roadmap stage 3 (see F32), asking what to do next, planning the week, or reaching this stage's exit] Stabilize: 1 to 4 employees, owner as trainer; fix top complaint, nurture, qualification, onboarding, first hiring funnel, payroll and collections. (rules-stages.md)
- S4 [at roadmap stage 4 (see F32), asking what to do next, planning the week, or reaching this stage's exit] Prioritize: 5 to 9 employees; niche down to the best clients, V2, rep KPIs, CS metrics, handbook, P&L and cash flow. (rules-stages.md)
- S5 [at roadmap stage 5 (see F32), asking what to do next, planning the week, or reaching this stage's exit] Productize: 10 to 19 employees; second premium product for existing clients, lead scoring, game tape, support templates, fair exits. (rules-stages.md)
- S6 [at roadmap stage 6 (see F32), asking what to do next, planning the week, or reaching this stage's exit] Optimize: 20 to 49 employees; do everything better not more, one improvement process, ad assembly, security protocols, benefits and pay. (rules-stages.md)
- S7 [at roadmap stage 7 (see F32), asking what to do next, planning the week, or reaching this stage's exit] Categorize: 50 to 99 employees; sort everything into buckets, second channel, proactive journey map, recruiting and HR systems, budgets. (rules-stages.md)
- S8 [at roadmap stage 8 (see F32), asking what to do next, planning the week, or reaching this stage's exit] Specialize: 100 to 250 employees; dedicated teams, pruning, product paths, structured interviews, expense and people cleanse. (rules-stages.md)
- S9 [at roadmap stage 9 (see F32), asking what to do next, planning the week, or reaching this stage's exit] Capitalize: 250 to 499 employees; a big bet in people, product or brand, specialised reps and support, tidy up as if for a sale. (rules-stages.md)

### 3. Offers (O)
- O1 [designing or reviewing the offer] Make an offer so good the avatar feels stupid saying no; audit its five parts and track response, close rate and price per version. (rules-offers-1.md)
- O2 [positioning, branding, choosing partners, endorsers or public appearances, or comparing with competitors] Sell in a category of one: get narrower until something true is unique, protect the owner's name, keep a checkable facts-only comparison; content production style matches the price position, chosen on purpose; collaborations checked against the name's associations; lo-fi on a separate channel or brand only after the first target; a brand is chosen associations built by pairing; advertising makes known, branding is what is made known; count who each pairing gains and loses; one central pillar every avatar member wants; a small seller's true edge over the leader (the owner's own attention); differentiator the buyer can see; credibility first; associations through what the avatar likes; brand moments with a public goal and stakes; e-commerce: one brand around the winning product beats an unbranded portfolio; ; sell from the true position: small means attention, large means track record; ; brand judged on reach, influence and direction; outweigh a bad pairing; ; keep ownership of content and name; the personal brand changes with the owner; vet partners and advertisers on the family test, drop failing ones fast; consider not selling the product the business advises on; disclose paid ties; transfer the face of the marketing to a successor over about a year; keep the personal brand separate; brand strength is the premium over a near-identical alternative; reinvest it in product and brand; never copy a rival's identity or story, only skills and structures; real beliefs and interests that break the stereotype; limit consuming rivals' marketing. (rules-offers-1.md)
- O3 [writing the offer, bio, pitch, posts or messages] Use one positioning line everywhere, test clarity on outsiders, one big idea per piece, written to one reader; test the explanation on the least informed likely buyer until they get it at once. (rules-offers-1.md)
- O4 [writing copy, pitches or scripts] Sell the destination, not the journey: benefit ladder, "so what?", one modest claim plus one true mechanism, one high-contrast picture. (rules-offers-1.md)
- O5 [writing copy, messages or call scripts] The pain is the pitch: their words, specific moments, What-Who-When, a doable step with every risk line, weekly reading of their press. (rules-offers-1.md)
- O6 [designing the offer or writing copy] Win on speed, risk or ease; cost bold promises, test them on 5 to 10 calls, and run costly ones capped first; levers for speed (templates, preparing ahead, availability), risk (consistency of variables), ease (remove what clients say is hard); cheapest only as a whole-business design; lead with one; internal skill levels as a true selling point. (rules-offers-1.md)
- O7 [designing delivery or onboarding] Walk the client's journey, list every action they must take, and cut them one by one. (rules-offers-1.md)
- O8 [writing, stating or choosing any guarantee] Build a specific guarantee with teeth from their fears; conditions that predict success; full terms first and valid claims honoured; guarantee as a paid option; never set a guarantee condition chosen because almost no one will meet it (X3); e-commerce: in-use demonstration video, try-before-you-buy as a guarantee, product names in the buyer's search words and outcome. (rules-offers-1.md)
- O9 [a prospect raises a non-price objection] Solve it once, turn the fix into a bonus, and keep a rehearsed bank of answers. (rules-offers-1.md)
- O10 [designing the offer or its messaging] Cut what the buyer must believe to one internal belief sentence; settle the rest with free content, one piece per logged objection, old belief broken before the new one. (rules-offers-1.md)
- O11 [writing copy or answering doubts] Admit weaknesses first, then "but" and the matching strength; never lie or imply a bigger business.; too-good-to-be-true offers: open calls with the trade-offs (rules-offers-1.md)
- O12 [writing posts, emails or pages] Say who it is for and who it isn't, state the real commitment, polarise on method and fit, never people. (rules-offers-1.md)
- O13 [writing promotions, hooks or angles; or responses are dropping; or an offer is tiring] Keep the core offer and change the wrapper, in a fixed refresh order; use the dos list before calling it tired; peak days: a month's preparation, page built for the day, few best sellers, start-here first-time offer, real end date. (rules-offers-1.md)
- O14 [designing a lead magnet or free offer] Solve one narrow problem that reveals the next one the core offer solves; never the core problem free. (rules-offers-1.md)
- O15 [naming the offer, a promotion, a bonus or a lead magnet] Name with the MAGIC formula, run the say-it test, test names against a control, no unproven figures in names. (rules-offers-1.md)
- O16 [writing an offer doc or pitch] Five parts: outcome, weekly deliverables, investment, risk reversal, and a true reason to act now. (rules-offers-1.md)
- O17 [writing any call to action] Clear next step, reason and what happens next; each line sells only the next step; use the P.S. and escape routes well. (rules-offers-1.md)
- O18 [testing marketing] Test offer, headline, image, then path tools; keep the tool with the best completion rate and the loser as backup. (rules-offers-1.md)
- O19 [planning content, the website or emails] Give away the how free in scrambled order; clients pay for the full sequence and implementation.; teach how it works to sell, sell the doing (rules-offers-1.md)
- O20 [making content or videos] Effort goes before recording; one-to-many assets split time 40% research, 20% body, 20% headlines, 20% checking. (rules-offers-1.md)
- O21 [designing, reviewing or pricing the offer] Value equation: raise dream outcome and likelihood, cut time delay and effort; score each driver 0 to 1; buyer-side five-step pass (F34 on the buyer's journey: delete, simplify, speed up the buyer's steps, then likelihood and a real guarantee, then price against the next best option's total cost). (rules-offers-1b.md)
- O22 [improving the offer] Focus on the bottom of the equation: faster and easier is hard to copy. (rules-offers-1b.md)
- O23 [improving the offer or the client experience] Perception is reality: make progress visible and look for psychological as well as logical fixes. (rules-offers-1b.md)
- O24 [writing copy, messages or the offer] Channel desires the avatar already has, frame by status, and win on likelihood, speed and effort when outcomes match. (rules-offers-1b.md)
- O25 [writing the offer, messages or calls] Raise perceived likelihood with true proof, independent recognition and being easy to check.; proof recent, visual, plentiful, told from the pain; testimonial at peak satisfaction (rules-offers-1b.md)
- O26 [designing the offer or onboarding] Build an early emotional win and visible milestones; track days to activation. (rules-offers-1b.md)
- O27 [competing with free options, or writing about speed] Fast beats free: lead with speed against free alternatives. (rules-offers-1b.md)
- O28 [designing the offer or delivery] List every hidden cost to the client and cut or reverse it, including what other solutions make them start or give up; every AI step must beat their current way. (rules-offers-1b.md)
- O29 [deciding what to build or include] Triple down on what clients value, cut the rest, and justify any addition in writing first; pick rank-and-build or subtraction; keep-one and not-miss questions; remove for new clients first; test new items with a limited scheduled release to the list before building them out. (rules-offers-1b.md)
- O30 [building or rebuilding the offer] Build the Grand Slam offer in five steps; test the offer doc and an internal launch note before building. (rules-offers-2.md)
- O31 [step 4 of O30, or stuck for ideas] Think divergently with the delivery cube, then the 10x and one-tenth test; no cost judgement while listing; modest-cost, high-value ideas; service hours and days with reply speed; staffing against waiting trade-off; live most valued, recorded most scalable; base units to sell (products, services, access, media, risk, money); referral-only prompt. (rules-offers-2.md)
- O32 [step 5 of O30, or adding anything to the offer] Trim low-value items, stack the rest by problem with honest values, and cut needless choices; full offer or none: no rushed or shortened pitch; value only when the slot does not suit an offer. (rules-offers-2.md)
- O33 [a prospect gives a reason not to buy] Solve every perceived problem the way they need, then template the fix. (rules-offers-2.md)
- O34 [choosing delivery methods] Favour high-value, one-to-many assets; save one-to-one time for the biggest value adds. (rules-offers-2.md)
- O35 [presenting, launching or promoting the offer] Enhancers raise demand without changing the offer; keep supply below demand. (rules-offers-2.md)
- O36 [selling places, groups or the lead magnet] [Fixed] Scarcity from real caps only, stated with real counts; scarcity decides when, never whether; sell about a fifth of known demand so the next round sells out too. (rules-offers-2.md)
- O37 [any sales conversation, campaign or page] [Fixed] Urgency from true deadlines only; launch with Whisper, Tease, Shout; never let anyone in after a deadline; keep usual value at usual volume during a launch, launch messages on top and worth watching. (rules-offers-2.md)
- O38 [after the first target, F3] [stage: S3+] A tiny, hard-capped, very high-priced direct-access tier, treated as a test. (rules-offers-2.md)
- O39 [building or presenting bonuses] Stack named, priced, proven bonuses that each kill a reason they can't succeed and are each worth the price alone; one or two bonuses for problems that only arise on success. (rules-offers-2.md)
- O40 [deciding what's core and what's a bonus, or building assets] Pull standalone high-value items out as bonuses and keep a recorded bonus vault; by-products turned into near-free bonuses that bring clients back. (rules-offers-2.md)
- O41 [adding bonuses from other businesses] Get exclusive perks from adjacent partners, rank deal types, disclose commission, review partners each group. (rules-offers-2.md)
- O42 [choosing or adding a guarantee] Guarantee menu: payout types and guarantee types with when each fits; price cap on estimates. (rules-offers-2.md)
- O43 [building or improving the offer] The 10x and one-tenth test; start high-touch, then move results into low-cost assets. (rules-offers-2.md)
- O44 merged into O39 and O41. (rules-offers-2.md)
- O45 [a prospect hesitates over an unconditional guarantee, or choosing guarantee types] Scripts for guarantee hesitation, extra guarantee types and stacking options. (rules-offers-2.md)
- O46 merged into O13. (rules-offers-2.md)
- O47 [writing or checking any claim in copy, calls or pages] Keep one sourced claims file, check every claim and figure before anything goes out, and correct errors the same day. (rules-offers-2.md)
