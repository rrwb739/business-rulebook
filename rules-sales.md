## Sales (C)

- C1 [a prospect shows buying interest] [Fixed] Close on a call. Send the offer document and payment link straight after the yes, never before the call for the prospect to "review with the others". When they're ready, stop selling and take payment: go straight to the agreement and the payment set-up, and read the key details back before hanging up (legal name, account holder, invoice email) so the first collection doesn't fail. In person, a payment method may be taken before the decision only with the true reason said plainly (so paying is quick if they go ahead), stored by a compliant payment provider, never charged without a separate yes to the amount, and deleted if they don't buy; never under a pretext, and no copy of an ID document unless the work truly needs it (see X8, X9). Put the agreement in writing every time, with the same line: so there are no unspoken expectations. When scope or terms are agreed in writing after a call, include the owner's photo, a one-line true bio (F21) and one short true personal line. Have the decision maker type their own details into the agreement (legal name, the named owner of the work on their side, invoice email), never in a way that discourages any exit right (O36, X3). If the owner quotes a price, bonus or term in error and the buyer says yes, honour it for that buyer and fix the template: never correct a quoted term upwards after a yes, and check the offer document against the call sheet before sending. If asked to "send over a proposal", agree, then book a time to walk through it with every decision maker, never leaving it to be read cold, and log that walkthrough as the next step (C11). Before the first call that could close, run a live end-to-end payment test (agreement, payment form and link, a small test payment refunded) and see it land in the business account; after each sale, check the agreement and amounts are entered correctly in billing.
- C2 [setting up the sales process] [Fixed] Before calls, send a short video answering common questions in the prospect's words, and ask qualifying questions: the size of their problem in the avatar's own unit, who handles it now, whether the problem is big enough that they could reasonably pay (without quoting the price yet), who decides, timing (a date they name for having it fixed), how much of their team's time they could give (confirm size and time as a pair when both are needed for the result), and who on their side will own the work afterwards (no owner is a red flag). Screen every prospect against the F12 red flags. Add questions that put a cost on the problem and project it forward. Invite every decision maker to the call; if one can't come, move the call. Before the price step, confirm by name who signs (L8) and when the owner last spoke to them directly, and give the senior buyer the business-level value and the day-to-day user the daily-work value, with the same facts and price (M2). When the enquirer isn't the decision maker, treat it as a buying signal: give the help they asked for first, ask what made the decision makers want it looked at now and what they'd need to see, reach a decision maker only with the enquirer's agreement or a three-way introduction, and tag pre-call clips by role. Pre-call video, so the call can go on their concerns rather than a pitch: (1) promise, pain, proof and plan in under 30 seconds, with the owner's name and one-line role as a caption so the spoken intro stays under 5 seconds; (2) the mechanism as old way against new way, or the hidden problem behind the visible one (O4), then the three pillars in 60 to 90 seconds; (3) the top 5 objections, each looped through the C14 belief-breaking formula, including "why hasn't this been solved yet?" and "why this beats the alternatives" (O2); (4) what happens on the call, who will ring, and one small, easy question to answer before the call; then proof, stacked, true only (O11, O47; until client results exist, the owner's own true results, F21). Put a new hit (a proof point, a specific pain moment, a surprising true fact, a short true story) every 20 to 30 seconds, timed on the draft and checked against viewer drop-off. Open every clip with the same 10 to 15-second line (name, who it's for, the one promise, who it isn't for), checked at D15; title clips as real buyer questions or true curiosity lines with no figures (O15); end each teaching segment by naming the next problem the core offer solves (O14); make the last clip a "you're probably wondering" list from the L59 log, each answer a checked claim (O47). Send a one-page written summary with the video. Keep it visibly current and re-record when its time reference passes; never imply a recording is live or present a clip as anything but a sales asset (see X9, X14). Pre-call form: honest, useful work, always presented as the first step of the diagnostic for a sales call, never as a survey (F25); ask two or three questions first (also for the D8 count questions), then send the fuller form once they've answered, with a personal named line and a thank you, never a bare link; ask about the problem from several angles (size, who handles it, how long, what the next 5 years look like if nothing changes). Before any one-to-many asset, fill in a one-page brief: the category of one (O2), the one belief (O10), the stack (O32), the true origin story (F21) and the three beliefs (vehicle, internal, external), each with a true story; each quarter, sort the C14 false-belief list into those three columns and check the clips answer the top one in each. In the O20 checking pass, read each clip against: does this make sense, could the buyer do this, do they need it; does it back their goal, credit past attempts truly, answer fears with evidence, confirm only true suspicions and name a problem, never a person, as the enemy. Build the first full video from the pitch as delivered live on about 20 calls. Read viewer drop-off: fix the biggest drop first and expand the parts people rewatch; if a recorded asset isn't converting, deliver that part live for a while, then re-record. Tests (L63, L15): the pre-call form against the short form on show and close rate, watching bookings; versions differing only in their examples; 3 or 4 short clips, one per key belief, the last openly asking them to book, against the single video on views to bookings; two opener sets for the buyer camps; the order hook, one belief, origin story, three belief stories, booking. Prioritise those ready now. Where a first sale opens an upsell or menu conversation (M32), send a second, separate short video before that conversation, about what buyers usually don't know about the rest of their situation, with true proof from similar clients, so the conversation starts framed.
- C3 [a lead books a call] [Fixed] Contact them straight away; offer an earlier slot if possible; send a small personal message before the call to raise the show rate. If they're free when the owner rings and every decision maker can join, run the call there and then; otherwise qualify briefly (C2) and book the earliest slot that works for all of them. On the booking or qualifying ring, clarify, label and size the problem only, with no solution, so they arrive wanting it. Show-up often moves sales more than call skill, so fix it first: below about 70% show, show rate is the problem, and a low rate can mean the wrong people are booking (L15). Default to calls the same or next working day from fixed call windows on set days (F23), never more than 3 working days out except to reach the next set call day; when there's a gap, ring those booked days ahead and offer an earlier slot, saying a slot has opened only when one truly has (O11). Offer times the buyer can take within the owner's set hours (F1), avoid the buyer's known busy periods, use 15-minute start times, offer "ring me now" alongside the link, open as many slots as the hours allow and favour slots with nothing straight after them. Never block out fake slots or signal idle capacity: offer two specific times, and say the week is filling only when logged as true (O36). Put the owner's name and photo on the booking confirmation with who will call and from which number, use the same clear, recent, unedited photo and sender name on every channel, and host the C2 pre-call video and one true proof line on the confirmation page. Dress as the buyer would for a client meeting, never props implying a role not held: looking credible never stands in for proof (O25). If anyone else calls leads, show that person, chosen on skill (see X18). End every booking with "if anything changes, will you let me know?" and wait for the yes; on a phone booking, ask them to put it in their diary on the line and read the time back. A free preference question before the call lifts show-up. Reminders: a personal message 24 hours before that shows prep (one observation about their business), then the morning of, then an hour before; each sells one reason to turn up (rotating pain, proof, speed, ease, with the dream outcome in their words) and never mentions how many people miss calls; voice notes or video only on a channel they've already used; anything automated must look automated and never pretend to be the owner; reminder texts are service messages with no pitch (see X2). The booking form asks for a mobile and a preferred reminder channel. Leave voicemails by hand (X2). If booked more than 2 days out, at most one touch a day until the call, and extra assets only when they reply or ask. No gifts to get them to turn up. Ring a no-show the same day: assume something came up, one light line at most, a specific new slot, no guilt; one who doesn't rebook gets the pre-call clips one at a time, at most one a day, then falls under L12. Move unconfirmed bookings with honest wording, never a made-up excuse. Log show rate before and after each change (L30) and test (L63): a short video on the confirmation against photo only; a last service text 10 to 15 minutes before; one teaser line in a reminder; a shorter stated call length, always the length the call really takes (O11); and, only where the owner will truly do it, saying at booking what will be prepared for them before the call (a short audit or plan for their situation) and that it is walked through on the call, not sent ahead. Show rate rises from many small fixes rather than one trick: keep the list of what has been tried with its before and after numbers. When a follow-up call is booked on a call, send the invite while they are still on the line, ask them to accept it, and ask them to text if anything comes up so the slot can go to someone else (only if that is true). Availability is the biggest lever on how many leads book: offer as many call slots as the owner's set hours genuinely allow, across as many days as possible, in short start increments (15 minutes), and aim for same-day or next-day calls, since show rates fall the further out a call is booked. When a slot genuinely frees up, offer it to someone booked later. When someone misses a call, send one light, warm message the same day with no guilt (assume something came up), restate what is set aside for them only if true, and offer the same time the next day; if they miss again, move on.
- C4 [preparing for a sales call] Spend about 10% of the call's length on research, about 90% of it on the prospect (their pains, situation, past attempts) rather than the product, and reread the notes so they never repeat themselves. Use one research fact in the first minute and bring one pre-worked observation about their business; for a new, unknown supplier, prep stands in for reputation, so bring one worked idea inside the offer's scope, never advice the owner isn't qualified to give (X4). Research stays on the prospect's own business sources (website, directories, public registers, reviews, public professional roles), never profiles of named people built from social media (X2). At the start of any second call, read the notes back, re-run the short agenda and briefly re-clarify the problem in their words. For an unusual name, note a phonetic spelling, rehearse it, and if unsure ask once. For a rare, high-stakes meeting, prep for longer. Before a call with a dismissive or upset person, write down one true thing to respect about them or their business, and say it only if honest and relevant. Notes stay on the business and the decision: record exact words, actions and dates, never a guess at what they meant, mark any inference as an inference, and record personal details only if offered and relevant (see X8).
- C5 [on a sales call] [Fixed] Follow CLOSER: clarify why they're here, label the problem, overview past attempts, sell the destination, explain away concerns, reinforce the decision. Run the diagnostic inside it: they score where they are and their goal, and the price is tied to that gap. Listen twice as much as you talk. Run the full sequence on every call, warm and referred included, so a lost call shows which step failed (F24, C13). (1) Open: if they watched the video or used the lead magnet, start with what stood out; if not, offer to let them watch it now or hear the short version, never a condition of the call (M27). Ask one genuine, specific question from the C4 research, then give a 15 to 20-second agenda (proof, promise, plan, call length, "if it doesn't make sense, no problem, and you'll leave with something useful either way"), framed as a diagnosis: if the owner can't help, say so as soon as it's clear. The agenda may include whether they qualify for the guarantee or a bonus, on real criteria applied to everyone (O8, L2). End with "does that sound fair?" and wait (L47); at the stated time, ask whether to carry on or rebook. (2) Clarify: 2 or 3 true yes questions from their own reply, never a trick; one change they already pulled off; "what made you reply?", why now, why that goal; play their words back and get the problem confirmed. Ask one future question, then where they are today against it. Dig with "tell me more" and "can you give me an example?" until there's a specific moment with a number, then a ladder of why questions, stopping at business standing, never personal ground. Hard gate: no ask until they've said in their own words why they need a fix. By about minute 8 know their size, who handles it and the biggest pain, with nothing about the owner before that. Treat easy early agreement as a reason to dig. Offer no fix in discovery. Ask for counts, not adjectives (D8), and use corrected numbers without comment. Ask whether the goal is a must-hit and what a miss costs. The L34 magic question may come once the problem is labelled. (3) Label: "you're at X, you want Y, Z is holding you back", in their words plus the true cause or cost they hadn't put into words (O5). (4) Overview past attempts (what, how long, how it went, "what else?" until nothing's left), then what they already have and don't fully use (O2, O28). Credit what each attempt gave and name the missing piece, never running down named tools; free them from a past choice without loss of face, true only. Put a figure on the cost. Collect budget, authority and timing inside these questions. Ask "if we didn't speak again, what's the plan?" and judge it honestly: if it would work, say so (M27, C14). Write each roadblock in their exact words: the pitch uses only these. Note what they liked and disliked about each past attempt: link the offer to what they liked, and never lead with anything that resembles what they disliked. Bring an overconfident buyer back with one true question and steady an unsure one by normalising the doubt, logged as an inference (C4). Then ask permission to explain. (5) Sell the destination in 60 to 90 seconds, never over 3 minutes: the three pillars set in the instructions box, each with one statement, one analogy from their world, one commitment matching the guarantee conditions, mapped by name to a roadblock they gave, with one true proof example (O15; until client results exist, the owner's own, F21). Mention only features they care about; never hold back anything that would change their decision (M27). (6) Before the price, raise likely obstacles yourself, name and probe any hesitation, re-check budget, decision makers and timing, check priority ("if not, we'll stop here"), recap the pillars in their words and ask how things would go with all three running. Any big numbers before the price are true and relevant, never anchors (M27, O11). (7) Ask using their goal in their words: current state, goal, obstacle, path, price, then the saving. Let a neutral measure (their diagnostic or own log) show the gap; after a live demo on one case, point to how many more they have, then price (C7). Say what paying means, inside the guarantee terms (O8, O15). Make the last thing shown before each ask a true reminder of their gap or win. (8) Reinforce: after payment, restate proof, promise and plan in their words, book the kickoff and pass notes to onboarding (D7). With no decision or a missing decision maker, email a short recap in their words within a working day, asking them to correct it, with no ask (L30). Throughout: speak in questions, restatements and short stories; answer price, terms, exclusions and data straight. Never disagree; ask permission before a hard truth; use only true third-party foils where the owner lacks authority. Never tell them they're a good fit: ask until they say it, and decide privately against L2 and F12. Ask "what would you want to be sure of before deciding?", never "any questions?". Never say "ignore the price for now"; correct any misstatement in writing. Meet dismissal with one calm true line, and experience with one true respect line and the owner's narrow lane. Bring in the quietest person by name. Correct only facts that affect the decision, via the published source (X4, D16). With several decision makers, build more than one relationship and test a stated 60 to 90-minute working session with all of them (never the full offer sequence, O14; L63). Never use real client data in demos (X5). Record only with consent (see X8). Pre-handle the usual concerns inside the discovery questions rather than meeting them at the end: have they set money aside for solving this, has anyone who shares the decision seen it, have they bought something like this before and how did it go (C14). Never give away the full diagnosis and plan as free consulting before the offer; the call diagnoses, the offer prescribes. Once they say yes, ask one plain question to surface any hidden doubt, such as "apart from something unexpected, is there anything that would stop you starting?", and answer it there and then; this is a check, never pressure, and a real doubt is resolved or the sale is not taken (M27). If they ask for a proposal, ask what they'd need to see in it, answer that on the call, ask again, and if it still needs a document, book the next call before hanging up (C11).
- C6 [presenting bonuses on a one-to-one call] Ask for the sale first, on the core offer. If they say yes, reveal the bonuses after they've signed, to confirm the decision. If they say no, agree, add the one bonus that removes their stated obstacle and ask again whether that's fair; repeat with the next bonus if needed. Each bonus carries its genuine stated value (O39, X3) and removes a stated obstacle, never a shiny extra to blur a gap in the numbers, with the total shown in writing (M32). Never discount to close: adding value keeps a position of strength, while discounting teaches buyers that prices are negotiable. This order is for calls only: one-to-many assets stack before the ask (O32).
- C7 [presenting the price] [Fixed] Use the return close with their own numbers: "if this saved you X, worth Y a year, would [price] be worth it?" Get the cost from them first, as their own rough estimate, and use their number, never the owner's; work the sum live with deliberately cautious inputs, labelled as an illustration until client results prove it (O15). Before the price, close off "that was quick" once with a yes-framed line, only if true and never naming a time not yet measured, and keep the price on the value, never the owner's hours (M2). Give one true reason for the price, never implying a guaranteed result (O8, O15). M2 sets the price where the gap to the value is clear, so buyers feel it's a steal and stay and refer (L65). Just before the number, say plainly that it is expensive (or a serious investment), so the buyer hears it from the owner first and the owner never sounds apologetic; then state the price, stop talking; if the owner chokes on it, show it in writing on screen (M32). "That's expensive" is usually a reaction: agree and carry on. A money objection is usually a value gap: go back to the return close before any payment plan (M37). A run of calls with no flinch means the price is too low (M33). If the owner can't say the price with full belief, fix the offer, not the pitch (O1): reread client results (until they exist, the owner's own, F21) before every call block and after a run of noes, rewatch the owner's best calls, and book calls soon after a client win. Self-check: would the owner pay this in the buyer's place? When calls stall, ask whether conviction, expertise or rapport is low before changing the script. Never blame a client who didn't get the result: ask what would have made the next one succeed and fix that gap. Label any multi-year value illustration as such until sourced (see X9). Tests on 10 calls each (L63): a true pre-frame ("it isn't cheap") then a pause against the plain statement, watching the flinch rate; a loss-framed return close against the gain version, with their own cautious numbers (O13, O15).
- C8 [a prospect hesitates, or the call gets lost in detail] Best case, worst case close: best case, the problem is solved for good; worst case, the guarantee protects them (O8); the only sure loss is changing nothing. When a call gets lost in detail, say "let's zoom out" and go to best case, worst case.
- C9 [a prospect asks for more proof] [Fixed] Ask how much proof would be enough and whether that's reasonable, then find the real fear behind it. Raise the doubt first ("you're probably wondering whether this works for someone like you. I'd wonder too. What would you need to see?"), then meet that criterion if it's reasonable and true. Once a client agrees in writing, offer a short reference call with them (ideally outside the prospect's market), with no undisclosed reward to the referee (X3), no introduction not cleared, no deadline unless real (O37), and contact details shared only with consent (see X8). Once 2 or 3 have agreed, offer the list and let the prospect pick, never two direct rivals; for a sceptic, suggest a reference who had the same doubt. Lighter version: the prospect sends one written question to a consenting client, answered in the client's own words and passed on unedited, with any perk disclosed (X3), and the question logged under L59.
- C10 [a prospect says no or stalls] Resolve the concern, then ask again, as many times as each concern gets resolved, and only once the reason is found and resolved. Use a past client story and play the future out. Stack answers from different angles while rapport holds; if they close off or want to end the call, end it and book a follow-up (M27): whoever asks most sells most, but rapport sets the limit. Expect a first no; it starts the close rather than ending it. The objection playbook is C14.
- C11 [the end of any call] Book the next step before hanging up, and ask "who do you know who'd benefit from this?" Ask for the next step with low-pressure yes-framed questions ("would it help if...?"), and test a "would you be opposed to...?" version against it (L63). After a no or a "not now", one light ask for a three-way introduction is fine only if the call gave them something useful: ask once, never for a list of names, and drop it at the first hesitation (L66 consent and X2 apply before contacting anyone named).
- C12 [negotiating price or terms] [Fixed] Have an alternative first: whoever needs the deal less has the upper hand, so never look as if the owner needs the sale. Anchor first and high, but credible: an opening the other side sees as absurd loses good faith and its concessions aren't returned. Move in small steps, trade on terms (start date, payment timing, scope) rather than price, and offer two or three equal options. When asked "can you do it for less?", answer with the opposite question, "can you do it for more?", meaning what more would make it worth it to them, and if a lower price is still needed, take something real out of the scope or terms to match it, never the same thing for less (M2). Frame the price as an investment with a return. Every step down is a real trade, never a staged retreat from a position never held (M27, O11; see X9); when a real trade comes from the buyer's request, say so plainly, and log outcomes for those deals against the rest (L63). Name one small, true weak point in the terms early, then trade on what is strong. When another party proposes terms the owner can't accept, don't argue: say the owner would likely do the same in their place, set out one page of the true advantages and disadvantages of each option for them, and let them decide (O11).
- C13 [before a block of sales calls, or practising sales] Practise selling like any skill (F24), scaled to the owner's set hours: 15 to 20 minutes before each call block reading the script aloud with the tone marked, drilling replies to the top objections and reviewing part of one recorded call. Know the script and stories so well they come without thinking, so attention stays on listening; for objections, learn the reasoning per type and rehearse two or three answers in the owner's own words (C14). Fix one line at a time with 10 to 30 reps, then three clean runs. Train back to front: rehearse taking payment and the ask first. Role-play before going live, especially price, guarantee and top objections, with the partner told to stop the owner at the first slip and make them redo the line. Rehearse in call conditions (the real device, standing, the real call sheet and lead record, the real opener). Rotate each session through intro, discovery, pitch, close and objections, then the weakest step, about 10 reps each; a clearly broken step gets every session until fixed. Work on one skill for 1 to 2 weeks, chosen monthly from an L30 number and written down with the rep plan. Write a rubric for each CLOSER step (what a 1, 5, 7 and 10 look like), score each role-play and one recorded call a week against it, and once a month have someone outside the business who is ahead in sales (F15) score one role-play. Tone: questions rise, the ask goes slow and low, emphasis changes meaning, so mark it in the script and pick up the pace just before the close. Keep a one-line approach per common buyer type: same script, facts and price (M2), different pace and emphasis; match the buyer's pace and terms in the owner's own voice, never mimicking (W1). After each call, critique first, then what went well, then what to change. Score each recorded call against a yes or no checklist of every C5 step, count interruptions and fix the first missed step. Keep a "don't say" list of lines that lost momentum, each with its replacement, read before each block; start it with "I'll prove to you...", "let me show you why..." and opinion words such as "obviously" or "clearly" in discovery, and mark any sigh or tone that signalled disagreement. Once a week, role-play the hostile call (a hang-up, "how did you get this number?", "take me off your list", a dismissive buyer), answering every opt-out per X2. Add a drill-down role-play that asks ever deeper questions until the owner reaches the L16 "I'll check and reply in writing" line (X4). Practise on lower-scoring prospects inside the avatar, never outside it (L2), to the full standard and never prejudging who can pay. Keep the diary full so no call feels make-or-break. Before the first live block, run one concentrated practice week; after any F12 update or new offer version, a focused week on the changed lines. Rehearse the origin story and three belief stories weekly, each under 60 seconds (F13). Monthly, review that month's consented recordings, won and lost together, one question per CLOSER stage, and turn the patterns into script lines, don't-say entries and next month's skill (no client data, X5; see X8). On calls: stand and smile, keep self-view or a mirror, act as if when the feeling isn't there, and never let manner stand in for substance (C4, L16); when an objection lands, take one slow breath and a one-word reset ("huh, tell me more"); load the next lead between calls, never during; take 1 to 2 minutes of movement between calls; cap each block's length and keep research and admin out of it; log dials per hour (L30). Judge every call, the owner's or a rep's, first on adherence to the script and the C5 checklist, not on whether it closed: a close won by going off-script is a false positive and is never copied into the script, and a lost call that followed the script is a script problem, not a person problem. Change the script only once adherence is steady and the close rate still lags (L16). With a team, recognise adherence each day with the manager's specific approval and, if wanted, a small token reward paid whether or not the call closed, alongside pay for results (T1; see X18, X19). After a lost sale, ask what wasn't found out, never as a reason to push harder (M27). Never pose as a buyer to shop rivals' calls. Within the owner's set hours, practise curiosity in ordinary conversations (questions only, nothing logged about the person). Train new sellers first on recordings of good calls (recorded with consent, X8), then on a reduced call schedule, reviewing each call with the manager by asking the seller first what they would do better; in weekly team reviews peers name only what went well, and the manager drills one part of the script at a time, repeated until it comes without thinking, before moving to the next part.
- C14 [an obstacle, objection or stall on a call] [Fixed] Understand the reason behind each type rather than memorising closes, and keep two or three answers per type in the owner's own words, rehearsed (O9, C13). Five types: time, money, fit, other people and self (fear of choosing wrong, or not knowing how to decide). Expect several layers, each answer revealing the next; blame moves from outside in (circumstances, other people, self), so the layer shows how close the real reason is, and "I need to think about it" means the owner is now talking to the decision maker. Choose the answer by whether the hesitation is about the past, present or future, and answer on its own dimension (money with money, time with time, fit with fit). With any of them: curiosity first ("tell me more"), then acknowledge (saying their last phrase back is enough), associate (a true similar case or story, or "fair question"), then ask; never answer in the same breath, and mark this on the call sheet (L16). When stuck, slowly say their question back. If the owner wins the argument, the sale is lost. Never answer a fear with "don't worry": give one claims-file fact (O47), the real support terms (O8) and one true story. For a factual objection, agree fully with the condition first, then ask true yes questions from published sources or their own numbers so they reach the conclusion themselves, never a trick (M27). Ask "why?" until the underlying reason appears, then show how the offer serves it better; the reason given not to act is usually the reason to act. Belief-breaking formula, for the pre-call video, emails and calls: what many people think, why that's not quite right, what works instead, an example, then one plain line restating the new belief from the claims file (O47); on a call, said to the buyer's own view, use "in some cases that holds; here it looks different, because...". Examples, associations and quoted sources must be real, accurate and never imply endorsement (O11, O15, O25; see X9); name a past client only with written consent, otherwise describe them. Keep a story bank: one true story per objection type (consenting clients or the owner's own experience, never composite or invented), each a one-line plot (who, what they wanted, what stood in the way) plus where and how it felt, in a 30 to 60-second and a 2 to 5-minute version; grow it to 10 to 20 false beliefs from the objection log and L59, leaving a slot empty rather than invent one. Nudges: "you'll fix this eventually, so would you rather it pays off faster or slower?"; for perfectionists, "will this get you closer to your goal than what you're doing now?"; not deciding is itself a decision. After the price, light humour can point out a contradiction, then a serious "what's really going on?". Before the close they must want the goal and believe that this gets them there in the way they want, that the people around them will back it, and that it will work for someone like them. Answers by type: time is priority (everyone has the same hours, so ask how important this is and what it would replace); preference, wanting the result their own way, means changing the method's variables changes the outcome; money goes logic (is the result worth the price?), then proof from buyers as similar to them as possible, then payment terms; a stall gets "what are you afraid of having happen?", then three plain questions (more likely to succeed doing it than not? closer to the goal with help than alone? access to the money?), stopping at any honest no; a past bad experience gets "one bad provider doesn't make every provider wrong; don't let it cost you twice". Other people: where the purchase needs someone else, invite them to the call; otherwise ask what that person would object to and answer those points, and leave the decision with the buyer and their partner. Never press a buyer to decide without or against a partner who shares the money, and never use lines like "better to ask forgiveness than permission" or "you're asking for permission, not support" (see X9). A delayed payment or cooling-off period is offered only on real terms (X3). Ask questions rather than make statements when a concern comes up: a statement gives them something to argue with, a question lets them reason it through. Agree with whatever is true in the concern first ("you're right, it isn't cheap, and it's real work"), then ask what would make them feel completely sure, and meet those criteria only where the owner truly can. Stay relaxed and willing to walk away: if it isn't right for them, say so; a clear decision either way is a good call. A story of a past client in the same position is used only if it is real and consented (X13, O25).
