## Delivery and operations (D), continued

- D12 [running the core offer's delivery] [shape: service] Deliver the first emotional win in week one (O26), show it on the progress tracker (O23), and point out every short-term benefit they notice. Set short task deadlines inside each week for several visible wins and specific praise per week; never let good work by client staff pass without a word. Each group hears consented, anonymised wins from other clients, with no client personal data (X5) and no fee or customer details shared between clients. Give each weekly task and recording a one-line reason tied to their goal, true only (O11). At the end of weeks 1 and 2, the named lead writes a few lines in the tracker, in their own words, on what has changed: no reward, no need to be positive, private unless later published under M45 with written consent; reuse it at the follow-on call (M8, M40), and compare later task completion with groups without it. Rewards for tasks stay small and about the result (specific praise, recognition of the named lead), never big outside prizes (see the Notes on M30 and M38). Keep missed-action fees as small as the legitimate interest allows (X3), never the main reason tasks get done. Give the weekly reason as the team's own goal first; the guarantee conditions are stated clearly at kickoff and in the terms (O8), not used as the weekly push. Never rank clients or staff against each other: show progress against the client's own day-1 baseline, and suggest one weekly team number posted where staff see it beside last week's, with no comment. Group updates report how many have done a step, never who is behind; laggards are chased one to one (D13). Wherever a client sees itself against an average (L32), an above-average figure carries one plain line of approval, and published averages also say what the top clients did. Open each tracker with the steps genuinely done already ticked, never filler. Give each group one shared, true goal tied to a real date and celebrate the joint result. Deliver good news personally and fast, let the tracker carry weak numbers with the next step beside them, and name the part of the method in every win message. On the day a client's first workflow goes live, send its named staff a short personal note naming what they did (a card or message at most, M27). At milestone reviews, thank the named lead in front of their boss, with their agreement. In the client's busiest weeks, send only short, warm messages with no new tasks or asks, and hold reviews until the week after. The last session is a results round: each client states its day-1 and end counts (D8), recorded only with consent, with no client personal data (X5) and no fees or customer details (M12); it gives proof and leads into the follow-on call (M8). Show the named lead's D7 goal at the top of the tracker and reread it midway; after the opening report (D14), give one true short story tied to the week's task. Test after 3 paid clients: the named lead picks, from 3 true options, a plain label for what the team now does, never implying accreditation (O25; see X9), at most a desk card the same for everyone, judged on M9 retention by group.If attendance at group sessions is low, give each new client one or two one-to-one sessions before they join the group, book their next session in the last minutes of every session, and back each booking with an automatic reminder plus a short personal note from the session lead; measure attendance before and after.
- D13 [training a client's staff] [shape: service] Give fast, in-the-moment correction of the step, not the person: name the gap between what happened and the checklist step, in the D16 order, using stop, start, keep, and teach as "under this condition, do this". When a team has not done a task, assume good intent, say it lightly and confidently, then give the next step; never blame. Never agree with a staff member's "not techy" label: treat it as a missing step, give one small rep they can finish in the session, and praise it. In group sessions, praise specific actions in front of the group (naming people only with their agreement) and correct one to one in private, with the checklist step and an example. Once clients consent, record staff from more than one client (not the owner) running a workflow (X5) and show doubtful staff short clips several times in week 1: a peer model works better than the owner, and several beat one. Ask each client's named lead by name for their one action or question, never "any questions?" to the room, and give each client a private question route. Every named staff member gets one small part to say or do in each session; never skip the quiet or least confident person. When one staff member dismisses another, point to the shared deadline and show the team how to draw the quieter colleague out. Greet and thank by name the junior staff who do the work. When a client's draft misses, name what works, give 2 or 3 lines to redraft along, and edit their second version with them. When a workflow asks staff to drop a habit they are proud of, say what is good in it, say plainly it is a real change, tie it to their goal, leave it until the next session and praise the switch. When a client slips, cite its own best week as the standard, give the gap in its numbers and offer to find the fix together. When a client owns a miss in a group session, thank them, say misses are normal in a new workflow's first weeks and state confidence in the next step; the detail goes one to one. Introduce each new tool step as a skill staff already use. Suggest to the named lead that the most sceptical or fussiest staff member owns the step their trait suits, with a true reason; if a named lead is not working out, suggest privately to their boss a co-owner or a narrower role, never in a group; the client decides its roles. Open each session by asking a named person for the last step they got working (a count, D8); when someone doubts they can run a workflow, point to a true past change they handled and a step they have already done. Before any hard conversation (tasks not done, an upset client, a refund or cancellation, asking a pilot for full price, F4), open with the goal the client set in its own words at kickoff (D7), then the issue and the next step; true goals only, never a manufactured "we" (O11). For a doubtful long-serving staff member, show a clip of a similarly long-serving, once-sceptical person (consent, X5). On how a workflow is actually run, ask the person who does the work before their boss. Ask everyone to set their display name to first name and organisation. Take anything ambiguous or emotionally loaded by phone or video first, then a short written recap. In session one, say how corrections will come (the checklist step, one at a time, in private, D16) and how to use one: note the step, redo it, ask if unclear; a correction is about the step, never about them, and they should say if one landed badly. Start each workflow with the named lead plus the most respected staff member, let colleagues see it working on the first batch, then widen.
- D14 [any meeting, session or client conversation, or someone raises a problem] Communication that changes nobody's behaviour wastes both sides' time. Before or at the start, ask "what do you want to happen as a result of this?" and "what should we start or stop doing?". When someone raises a problem, let them finish and acknowledge it first if raised with feeling, then ask what change in behaviour would stop it happening again. If someone rejects something mid-session, thank them, say the owner will think it over and mean it, book the follow-up for the same or next working day and run the F27 questions first; a compliance error is still stated plainly on the spot (D16). If the owner does not understand what someone means, ask plainly, and seek clarity rather than agreement. Reduce any task or role to the few actions that drive results, then ask how to do more of them, faster or better. No standing status calls with a client: every session ends with changed actions, and ad hoc 15-minute calls are booked only to solve a named problem. Record each action as action, owner, hours: what it is, which named person owns it, how many hours it takes (ask for hours, not a day) and when it is due. End every session by recapping the actions in order, rereading the full list each time one is added, and ask each owner to say back what they will do and by when; then each owner types their own actions and dates into the shared tracker in their own words, and in group sessions says their next action to the group, only with their agreement (nothing on fees, customer lists or customer details, M12). When someone has competing work, ask how many hours each task takes and set the order: prioritising is the lead's job. When a team says it lacks hours for the tasks, explain what that means for its result, then ask how it could fit (swap a task, move a slot, who covers) before proposing a fix, and record the answer as action, owner, hours. Open every session with each named lead reporting, in their own words, what they did since last time and the result as a count; log it under D8 and, with consent, in the C14 story bank and L32 proof. When the owner raises a problem in a client's workflow, give the next step and owner in the same message; if the plan is not ready, raise it anyway and say when the plan will follow (a compliance error is never delayed, D16). In any session over about 60 minutes, recap the key points and actions at the midpoint as well as the end. Write each session as a timed plan (segments in minutes, each ending in an action), and after each session cut or shorten one segment that produced no action (D15). Test (L63) in pilot and milestone reviews, never sales calls (C5): open with a one-page written summary read silently for 3 to 5 minutes, then ask each person's first reaction, the most junior first and the most senior last, and compare actions agreed against reviews without it.When the business's own mistake costs a client time or money, a refund alone leaves them worse off: put it right and then some (the owner's time, an extra session, a personal apology), quickly and without being asked, and log it under L75 so it doesn't recur.When a client says the method didn't work, walk through each step with them against what was taught, find the step skipped or changed, and have them rerun it correctly before changing the method (F24). With a client who seems set on proving it can't work for them, ask plainly and without blame which matters more, being right or getting the result, and leave the choice with them; clients who switch often become the strongest advocates.
- D15 [once a fortnight while delivering] [shape: service] Compare one recorded sales call with that client's first session: was every promise delivered, what easy extras did they expect, and what should change in the pitch or the delivery (O29, M13, L65)? Once a quarter, check that every buyer-facing step (cold message, lead magnet headline, video opener, call agenda, kickoff, booking page, offer document, agreement message, payment page) uses the same promise, the same words and the same frame (L11), with the guarantee's name and the owner's photo at each decision point. Every 10 sessions, compare those with the most actions done by the next session against those with the fewest: do more of what the top ones share and less of what the bottom ones share. Log hours spent on the core delivery materials, and before each intake review every changed delivery asset on at least 3 separate days before using it (delivery assets only: outreach copy stays send before tweaking, L16, L50). Judge the core materials on logged hours (hundreds over the year, not tens, within F1 hours), with selling still first (F3, F29). Before the first pilot, write a one-page service standard (replies within the stated time, a recap within 24 hours, materials before each session, sessions on time, client data under X5), every stated time true (O8; see X9), and score delivery against it at each fortnightly review, treating a miss as a slip under L75. Two weeks before each kickoff and before any new workflow goes live, run a written go/no-go check (the owner can run it from the checklist alone, D5; small batch tested, D3; data processing agreement signed, X5; manual fallback written, D3) and fix any red item in that fortnight, never planning on a regulator or vendor slipping a date. Before pilots, run a made-up test client through a full cycle of the work in the common tools' free trials, logging where it hurts, with no real client data (X5), and before each intake try any new rule, software or tool change that touches the workflows on it first. Once there are paying clients, review weekly instead of fortnightly while any refund or complaint has arrived in the last 30 days. When complaints say "harder, slower or less supported than I expected", listen to the sales calls first: fixing what the sales script implies often removes the complaint without changing delivery or the close rate. Put support one click away inside the product or portal, and log where each support request starts; the page or step with the most requests is the next thing to simplify. Give the support team a tool that finds past answers to similar questions and suggests a reply, which a person checks, edits and sends (X5, X8).
- D16 [giving feedback or correcting anyone: client staff, their bosses, suppliers, later the owner's team] Correction order. A compliance error is stated plainly and promptly first (X4); the rest is about how. Begin with true, specific praise naming the exact action and why it matters to their result. Join praise and correction with "and", not "but". Mention a true slip of the owner's own where there is one. Ask questions rather than give orders: when a step is skipped, first ask whether it is awkward or does not fit their tools, then give its reason in their terms, then the next step; let them finish, and log the reason as a checklist fix (D5). Checklist steps stay exact; requests and changes go as questions, and nobody is left to learn from a slip that reaches the client's customers. Correct in private. Hints and questions suit senior or sensitive people; junior staff get the step said plainly and kindly, because a missed hint is not feedback (D5). Make the fault seem easy to fix with one small next step. Then say the one result needed and the support the owner will give; the same order works for a stalled pilot (F4) or a client going quiet (F12). Praise even the slightest real improvement, every time, specific and sincere. Give them a true reputation to live up to. When asking a client to change something, open with one true line of thanks, say what the current way costs them, ask "when possible", and end with their gain. Never invent the praise, the slip or the reputation (O11, M27).
- D17 [any login, account, payment detail or data the owner holds: leads, pilots, clients] Security basics, now (see X8). Multi-factor login and a password manager on every business account (email, domain, bank, payment provider, lead sheet, AI tools, booking tool); open every business account on the business email, never a personal one; give any helper their own least-access login (T2) and keep a written list of who can reach money, client data and the lead sheet; back up the lead sheet and client records weekly to a second place the owner controls (L36); before paying any changed bank details, confirm by phone on a number already held; keep software and devices updated; encrypt laptops, phones and any exported client or lead files. Aim for the project's country's baseline security certification (see X8). Number: accounts with multi-factor on, out of total, target 100%.
