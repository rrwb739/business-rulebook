## Legal and tax (X)

- X1 [any action, claim, offer, message, contract, hire or spend that the law could touch] [Fixed] Obey the law of the project's country, as named in the instructions box, for marketing, consumer, data, employment, entity and tax matters. Every claim, limit, deadline, review and testimonial must be true and provable before it is used. Use the block below for the project's country. When the project's country has no block, or the point is not covered by its block, say so plainly and stop before acting until the point has been checked. These rules are general guidance, not legal advice. Confirm with a qualified adviser.

### United Kingdom

- X2 [sending or planning any email, text, messaging app or other electronic marketing, or building or using a contact list] [Fixed] Follow PECR, as amended by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025. Messaging apps such as WhatsApp count as electronic mail, so the same rules apply. Unsolicited marketing goes only to corporate subscribers (limited companies and LLPs). Individuals, including unincorporated businesses and partnerships that are not LLPs, need consent first; if it can't be told whether an address belongs to a company or an individual, treat it as an individual. An employee's work email at a company counts as the company's. Every message identifies the sender, is never disguised, and carries an easy opt-out, including the first message. List emails, sequences to lead magnet takers and keep-in-touch updates go only to contacts who may lawfully be emailed (business addresses within PECR for partner prospects); regular emails go to the opted-in list only, each with the opt-out. Anyone who asks to stop is never contacted again by any channel, and recontact rounds never include anyone who said no or opted out (UK GDPR right to object). Check that a one-line confirmation sent after an opt-out counts as a service message, not marketing. Reminder texts before a booked call are service messages only, with no marketing content. Open-tracking pixels may need consent: check before using them. If a wrong date or figure went out in a batch email, send those recipients one plain correction with no pitch, and include the opt-out. Under UK GDPR a named person's details are personal data even at work: keep a legitimate interest assessment on file, record where each contact came from, tell people where their details came from, and keep one do-not-contact list honoured forever, keeping opted-out records on a suppression list rather than deleting them so new lists can be screened against it. Generic addresses such as info@ are not personal data. Scraped or bought lists need the same lawful basis and screening (X8). Live marketing calls follow X6. A business trading through a company shows its registered name, registered number, place of registration and registered office on business letters, business emails, order forms and the website (Company, LLP and Business (Names and Trading Disclosures) Regulations 2015, regs 24 to 25), and pays the ICO data protection fee unless exempt (X8). Confirm with a qualified adviser.

- X3 [designing, pricing or advertising any money model offer: free items, trials, terms, instalments, surcharges, reference prices, prize draws or subscriptions] [Fixed] Not legal advice; check before first use. Price: the price and its VAT basis (plus or including VAT) are stated the same way everywhere and never rise between quote and signature; every paid extra appears with the full total before the buyer signs, and no new charge is introduced after the price is agreed (DMCC Act 2024 Schedule 20 for consumers; Business Protection from Misleading Marketing Regulations 2008 and CAP Code 3.1 for business buyers). Free: call something free only if it is genuinely free under the free-offer rules, meaning the buyer pays nothing beyond the unavoidable cost of responding; the advert states every condition; never raise the paid item's price or cut its quality to fund the free item; never call anything a "free trial" when it needs a purchase or carries fees (CAP Code 3.23 to 3.26, which also cover marketing to businesses); say "start without paying if you complete X", with the conditions in full. Terms before commitment: every fee, condition and cancellation term is shown in writing before the buyer gives payment details or signs, and initialled where onerous; fees for missed actions or early exit must protect a legitimate interest (onboarding cost, a discount given back) and not be out of all proportion, or a court can strike them out as penalties (Cavendish v Makdessi). Instalments and credit: offer instalment plans only to buyers that are companies or LLPs; an individual, a self-employed person trading alone, or a partnership of two or three partners counts as an individual under the Consumer Credit Act, so sell to them upfront only or take legal advice first (a higher total for paying later can count as a credit charge); no introductions to finance providers, because credit broking can need FCA permission. Surcharges: no percentage "processing fee"; consumer cards can't be surcharged at all, commercial cards only up to the direct cost. Reference prices and values: "valued at" and "save" claims must rest on prices genuinely charged or honest replacement cost, and a price rise before a "get X free" offer must be permanent, apply to everyone and come well before the offer, otherwise nothing is called free. Reviews and testimonials tied to an offer follow X12 and X13. Prize draws: free entry, published rules (closing date, how the winner is picked, whether more than one prize can be won), the prize genuinely awarded, and never imply that non-winners won something (Gambling Act 2005; CAP Code section 8). Subscriptions: the DMCC Act subscription contract rules protect consumers; with business buyers apply their spirit anyway, with clear renewal reminders and an easy exit; with consumers, follow them in full once in force. Existing terms: clients keep what they were sold until their term ends. Contacting past clients, entrants or competitors' unhappy clients follows X2 and X8. Confirm with a qualified adviser.

- X4 [any offer, deliverable, bonus or advice that sits next to regulated professional work, or any decision on investing, hedging or raising outside money] [Fixed] Never give regulated professional advice (such as legal, financial or tax advice) or do regulated work, such as preparing or submitting filings to a regulator, that the owner is not authorised for. Where the offer sits next to regulated work, the client stays responsible for its own regulated work and submissions. Never promise to cover a client's regulatory penalties, or errors in work the client is responsible for (X11). Never offer contract wording or data processing agreements as a bonus: they are legal documents and outside the business's remit. Investment decisions for business surplus are taken with advice from an FCA-regulated adviser, and hedging only with regulated advice; the rulebook's question lists are not investment advice. Any approach for outside capital must respect FSMA 2000 s21 on financial promotions. Confirm with a qualified adviser.

- X5 [any work, tool, recording, post or incident that touches a client's personal data] [Fixed] Client personal data stays in the client's own systems or in tools covered by a data processing agreement. The client is the controller: sign a data processing agreement before any work that touches real client data (UK GDPR). No client personal data in the owner's own tools, posts or recordings; recordings of sessions, workshops and interviews are made only with consent and contain no client personal data. A personal data breach involving a client's data goes to the client, as controller, without undue delay (to check). Before admitting anything that could become a claim (a data incident, a client's loss), tell the professional indemnity insurer and take advice first. Confirm with a qualified adviser.

- X6 [making or planning any live marketing, follow-up or partner call] [Fixed] Screen every number, including a published business number, against both the CTPS and TPS and the business's own do-not-call list before calling. Say who is calling, show a genuine number that can be called back (never withheld; Ofcom rules), and give a contact address if asked. Never use automated or pre-recorded calls or voicemail drops without specific consent; voicemails are left by hand (PECR). Repeated or abandoned calls fall under Ofcom's persistent misuse policy: keep call cadences within it, with at most one immediate redial of an unanswered call. Scraped or bought numbers need the same lawful basis and screening (X2, X8). Confirm with a qualified adviser.

- X7 [sending any letter, mailing or physical insert to prospects] [Fixed] Never send anything with a demand for payment for unsolicited goods or services (Unsolicited Goods and Services Act 1971). Never make an envelope or letter look like official or government post; keep it identifiable as marketing (CAP Code rules 2.1 and 3.1). Named recipients of a mailing are personal data: have the UK GDPR source notice and legitimate interest assessment ready before sending (X2). Any insert or gift follows X16. Confirm with a qualified adviser.

- X8 [collecting, storing, sharing, recording or using anyone's personal data: leads, prospects, candidates, staff, attendees or referees] [Fixed] Follow UK GDPR. Forms (opt-in, booking, data): say who the business is and how to contact it, give a privacy notice saying what the data is used for, keep marketing consent separate where needed, mark optional fields and explain why each field is asked; fields a payment scheme requires may be compulsory. Sources: bought or scraped lists are checked for a lawful basis and the electronic marketing rules before use (PECR, X2); personal data from public registers, websites, social media, press or rich lists is still personal data, so marketing to a person from it needs a lawful basis (a written legitimate interest assessment) and a privacy notice at first contact or within a month at the latest (Article 14), and the cost of notifying alone does not make notice disproportionate; when contacting a person named by a colleague, say truthfully who pointed you to them and where their details came from, and check whether naming the colleague needs their consent; source candidates through anyone's connections only after a UK GDPR and platform terms check. When anyone asks where their details came from, tell them the specific source there and then, and whether it was a public source; never make it conditional on a meeting, a purchase or anything else (Articles 14 and 15). Minimisation: for receptionists and other gatekeepers log name and role only; call notes record personal details only if the person offered them and they matter, record exact words and mark inferences; never record special category data (religion, politics, health and the like) about prospects, and never target ads by it. Recording: record calls and interviews only with informed consent, saying at the start that the call is being recorded and why, with a set retention period; analysing recordings with AI also needs transparency. Sharing: share anyone's personal details (introductions, pairings, attendee lists, testimonials, a reference client's contact details, hosted event guests) only with their consent, and keep personal details beyond the business result out of published proof. A founder story or early-stage log holds no prospect or client names, emails or screenshots. Never monitor individual client staff (screen, keystroke or per-person activity logs); that is the client's decision as employer, under the ICO's employment monitoring guidance. Records and decisions: keep a map of where personal data sits (Article 30 records), and keep a human review of any automated rejection by an applicant tracking system (Article 22 and ICO guidance). Suppliers: a supplier (including an offshore one) that handles lead or client personal data needs a written contract, a processing agreement and, for transfers abroad, a lawful transfer mechanism, in place before switching supplier. Security: Article 32 requires appropriate security for personal data already held: multi-factor login, least-access logins, backups, updated software, and encryption of laptops, phones and exported client or lead files; Cyber Essentials is the UK baseline certification to aim for (to check). Fee: once the business holds personal data, such as a lead list, check whether the ICO data protection fee is due, and include it in the annual bills reserve. Client data in delivery follows X5. Confirm with a qualified adviser.

- X9 [writing or approving any claim in copy, sales, a lead magnet, a demo, an event, a service standard, a label or client material] [Fixed] Every claim in copy has documentary evidence, held before use, and a recorded evidence status (CAP Code 3.7). In sales, state the facts and tell the truth. Objective claims (results that come "easily", a measurable result in a time frame, a time figure in copy or onboarding, a time in a service standard such as reply or recap times) must be measured and true, never an estimate presented as fact; never imply a guaranteed result that cannot be guaranteed. Loss-framed and gain-framed lines use true figures only. Popularity claims ("most clients", "fastest growing", "most popular") come only from dated, documented data; "even businesses like X use it" only with evidence and their consent. "New" only for something first offered within about the last 12 months (CAP advice). Never say the offer is "the only way"; "can't be done" or "only" claims only if literally true and evidenced, never resting on misleading ambiguity. Headlines and bullets must be true and answered where they say. Results, examples and stories used in sales must be real and substantiated, never invented or composite; value illustrations are labelled as illustrations until there is a source; results from hand-picked or unpaid pilots are labelled as pilots and not presented as typical for paying clients until paid clients confirm them; demos on made-up data are labelled as demos, never as client results; peer figures used as proof are sourced or are the business's own counted, dated data. Official status: names, headlines, categories, scores, method maps, team labels, completion certificates and client materials are labelled as the business's own and never imply official rules or requirements, an official or industry standard, accreditation, a qualification not held, or endorsement by a regulator, government or professional body; qualifications are stated exactly (CAP Code 3.1, 3.7). Claims about official dates, rules and penalties are checked against the primary source and never imply a rule or penalty applies before it does, or a way round official rules. Comparisons: keep rivals unnamed in public copy, or follow the CAP Code comparative advertising rules (3.33 onwards); never imply rivals lack something they also have, and never present a legal requirement (such as a data processing agreement or UK GDPR compliance) as a special feature. Never claim the offer is greener or paperless-friendly without evidence (CAP Code section 11, CMA Green Claims Code). Live and staged: a recorded asset never implies it is live; at any event, webinar or members' space, no planted attendees, no pre-arranged questions or testimonials presented as spontaneous, and no friends posing as buyers. Negotiation concessions are real trades, never a staged retreat from a price never genuinely held. Research interviews are never a disguised sales pitch ("sugging", direct marketing under PECR): say up front that a sales conversation may follow. Consumers are protected by DMCC Act 2024 Schedule 20, business buyers by the Business Protection from Misleading Marketing Regulations 2008 and CAP Code 3.1; an untrue statement that induces a contract can be misrepresentation, and selling what the business can't deliver breaks consumer and advertising law. Have a solicitor audit all live marketing, or use the free CAP Copy Advice service as a first step, and build the compliance checklist from that audit (to check). Confirm with a qualified adviser. No aggressive practices with consumers: no harassment, coercion or undue influence, including pressing a buyer to decide without or against a partner who shares the money (Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024, section 228); confirm with a qualified adviser.

- X10 [stating any limit, cap, place count, deadline, expiring bonus or reason to act now, on any page or call to action] [Fixed] False scarcity and false urgency are misleading marketing (X9). Every limit, cap, place count, capacity claim, reason to act now and deadline must be true, including on thank-you and booking pages; bonuses truly expire on the stated date, and a stated deadline is kept. Confirm with a qualified adviser.

- X11 [offering or wording any guarantee, anti-guarantee, exclusion or promise to pay] [Fixed] Under the CAP Code, state every guarantee condition clearly and give the full terms before the buyer commits, never imply a guaranteed result that cannot be guaranteed, and honour valid claims promptly. With a conditional guarantee, never call either choice risk-free: state the conditions in the same breath (CAP Code 3.1). An anti-guarantee ("all sales final") never removes the client's remedy if the service is not performed with reasonable care and skill (Supply of Goods and Services Act 1982 s13); any exclusion must be reasonable under the Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977; check before use. Never offer to cover a client's regulatory penalties or errors in work the client is responsible for (X4); any promise to pay damages is a guarantee that needs full terms. Selling a guarantee or extra cover: never charge for, or present as a special feature, rights buyers already have in law (a banned practice under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024, Schedule 20); and a paid warranty or cover against breakdown, damage or loss, especially one provided by a third party or going beyond the seller's own duties for its own goods or work, can be a contract of insurance that needs FCA authorisation (FCA Perimeter Guidance PERG 6.7; Re Digital Satellite Warranty Cover, Court of Appeal 2011), so check the perimeter before selling any. Confirm with a qualified adviser.

- X12 [asking for, rewarding, publishing or responding to reviews] [Fixed] Reviews must be genuine (DMCC Act 2024, CMA fake reviews guidance CMA208). Invite every client to review and never publish only the good reviews: cherry-picking is treated as misleading; label chosen quotes as testimonials. Never make a free service, bonus or other incentive conditional on a positive review, case study or progress post; disclose any incentive, and say clearly where a reviewer got the service free. Platforms that ban incentivised reviews get none, so incentivised testimonials go only on the business's own pages, labelled. Never pay or pressure anyone to remove, bury or suppress genuine reviews or posts, and never post or commission counter-reviews. Ask a platform to remove a post only if it is fake or defamatory. Report a rival's fake reviews, actor testimonials or false scarcity privately with evidence to the ASA, the CMA or the platform. Confirm with a qualified adviser.

- X13 [capturing, editing or using a testimonial, case study, client post or illustrative person] [Fixed] Testimonials and case studies must be true, genuine, held on file with the client's written consent and current (CAP Code 3.45 to 3.47); consent must cover use in ads. Never draft wording for a client to sign (CMA208). A clip cut from a testimonial must keep the speaker's meaning, and any objective claim in it needs documentary evidence (CAP Code 3.7). Run a client's or partner's post as an ad only with their written consent, labelled as an ad, with any payment or gift disclosed (X14). No actors, composites, AI-generated faces or voices, or stock photos presented as clients, and any illustrative person is labelled (DMCC Act 2024 Schedule 20 for consumers; BPMMR and CAP Code 3.1 for business buyers). Confirm with a qualified adviser.

- X14 [publishing any ad, sponsored content, guest post, clip, affiliate promotion or comment that promotes the business, or any content mentioning another business the owner is connected to] [Fixed] Marketing must be obviously identifiable as marketing (CAP Code rule 2.1): native-looking ads are fine, disguised ones are not, and sales clips and videos are never presented as anything else. Anything given in return for a link, share or mention is labelled as an ad. Guest content never implies endorsement. Comments stay within each platform's self-promotion rules. Affiliates, and anyone paid to post the business's content on their own accounts (including clippers paid per view), must label their promotions as advertising, and the business shares responsibility for that, so a clip without the label is never approved or paid. When the owner features or refers to another business they are commercially or personally connected to (as a shareholder, director, adviser, ambassador, through a commission or discount, or because family or friends own it), the content is advertising and says so upfront with a clear label such as "Ad" on the content itself; a bio, a tag, a discount code or an affiliate link alone is not enough. Never endorse what the owner hasn't used or checked, and hold any connected business to the same proof standard as the owner's own offers (L32) before promoting it: the audience's trust is worth more than any one commission (L75). Confirm with a qualified adviser.

- X15 [setting up or using any referral incentive, commission chain or referral charity donation] [Fixed] Disclose any referral incentive wherever a referral is public, and tell an introduced prospect what, if anything, the referrer gets. Get consent before contacting a referred person who wasn't introduced directly (X8). If a client that is a regulated professional would be paid for referring its own customers, its professional ethics code may require their explicit written consent, so keep such clients out of commission chains. Before offering a charity donation per referred sale, check the Charities Act 1992 commercial participator rules and any professional body's inducement rules; disclose it wherever public and never tie it to a review (X12). Confirm with a qualified adviser.

- X16 [giving or accepting any gift, insert or hospitality with prospects, partners, clients or their staff] [Fixed] Nothing beyond token value is given to or accepted from prospects, partner prospects, clients or their staff, and any gift is logged. Inserts, gifts and event hospitality stay modest, the same for everyone and never conditional on buying (Bribery Act 2010). Some professional ethics codes that bind the client also limit gifts that threaten objectivity. Confirm with a qualified adviser.

- X17 [designing any affiliate, partner or reseller pay structure] [Fixed] Avoid any structure where affiliates are paid mainly for recruiting other affiliates (UK trading scheme and pyramid rules). Affiliate labelling follows X14. Confirm with a qualified adviser.

- X18 [hiring, paying, managing, changing or ending anyone's work for the business: staff, contractors, freelancers, interns, callers, hosts or setters] [Fixed] Follow UK employment law, with advice taken first. Status: before paying any contractor, freelancer, host or paid helper regularly, check their tax status with the tax authority's employment status tool and, separately, their employment rights status (employee, worker or self-employed) against GOV.UK guidance; IR35 applies only when they work through their own company. Pay: at least the National Minimum Wage, with any commission or output pay on top; no unpaid or commission-only agents; interns doing real work get the National Minimum Wage; deductions need prior written agreement (including a device return policy) and never take pay below the minimum wage; a staff referral bonus is paid through PAYE with National Insurance, with its terms in the contract or referral policy; when switching to performance pay, existing staff keep their current pay with performance pay on top. Contracts: give each employee a written statement of particulars by day one, separate from the handbook; contract and role changes need consent; signing or relocation bonuses and any clawback are written and reasonable; long-term bonuses, profit share, share options, course funding or pay-to-quit offers are written, the same for everyone in the role, on top of minimum wage cash pay, with legal and tax advice first (EMI rules, settlement agreements). Employer set-up: before the first employee, register as an employer and run PAYE through RTI, with workplace pension auto-enrolment as the legal floor for benefits; employers' liability insurance is compulsory from the first employee; carry public liability cover too, with optional cover for employment claims; HR records cover PAYE, statutory holiday and Statutory Sick Pay. Policies: the handbook covers disciplinary and grievance procedures under the ACAS Code, the Working Time Regulations, statutory leave and an Equality Act 2010 section; write a health and safety policy once there are 5 or more employees (Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 s2(3)). Hours: never require hours beyond the Working Time Regulations 1998 limits, including rest rules and weekend or evening shifts, and consider flexible working requests properly. Selection: selection, adverts and trial tasks use job-relevant evidence and observed behaviour only, never appearance, demographics or protected characteristics (Equality Act 2010), including for anyone chosen to call leads or represent the business. Before hiring a rival's team, check restrictive covenants, non-solicitation terms and confidential information. Leaving: a fair capability process (clear feedback, formal written warnings, a reasonable improvement period, never peer pressure alone) under the ACAS Code comes before anyone leaves for performance; roles found unneeded go only through fair redundancy (fair selection, consultation, notice and statutory redundancy pay where due); a resignation clearly given stands; final pay includes accrued holiday and notice, with a P45. Confirm with a qualified adviser.

- X19 [setting tax reserves, claiming expenses or reliefs, or offering prizes, rewards, benefits or share options] [Fixed] Set the tax reserve rates on each client payment, and any tax planning, with a qualified tax adviser, and plan within the law during the year. Expect to owe tax on profit: through Self Assessment (on top of any employment earnings taxed through PAYE) if self-employed, with payments on account only if the tax authority's thresholds are met; or corporation tax if a limited company, where corporation tax payments on account apply only to large companies. Save for tax as each payment lands. Keep every receipt, including from before trading starts; claim only the business share of dual-use costs under the wholly and exclusively test; track allowable expenses and capital allowances through the year. Claim R&D relief only through a qualified adviser, under the merged R&D scheme. Check the tax treatment of contest prizes, staff rewards and share options (EMI rules) before offering them; private medical cover is a taxable benefit in kind. (UK, to check.) Confirm with a qualified adviser.

- X20 [choosing or reviewing the legal form, dealing with rivals or members who compete, chasing late business payments, holding cash, or protecting systems and brand] [Fixed] Legal form: decide it with a qualified adviser before the first invoice, trading as a self-employed individual or as a company limited by shares, weighing a limited company's liability shield against its filing duties (annual accounts, confirmation statement, corporation tax return); later review the structure (share classes, a shareholders' agreement or a holding company). Competition: never agree prices, fees or which customers each business targets with a rival, and where members or clients may compete with each other, never share their prices, fee levels, customer lists or customer details between them at events, meetups or in members' spaces (Competition Act 1998); a disclosed referral swap is fine. Late payment: check the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998 before using or waiving statutory interest on a late business payment. Cash: keep idle business cash in interest-bearing easy-access accounts spread across banks within the FSCS limit. Systems and brand: check the security protocols (firewall, encryption, multi-factor login, patching, access control) against Cyber Essentials (X8), and run a UK IPO trademark check before listing IP, trademarks and domains on the balance sheet. (UK, to check.) Confirm with a qualified adviser. Exclusive supply or distribution deals, pricing below cost to keep rivals out, and buying competitors can break competition law once a business has market power or a deal is large enough for merger review (Competition Act 1998 Chapter II; the CMA's merger thresholds); check with an adviser before any of them. Confirm with a qualified adviser.

- X22 [selling to consumers at their home or door to door, or agreeing any consumer contract away from the business's premises] [Fixed] UK doorstep and off-premises selling. Before the contract, give the consumer the required pre-contract information (including the price, the trader's identity and address, and the 14-day right to cancel with a cancellation form) on paper or another durable medium, and a copy or confirmation of the signed contract (Consumer Contracts (Information, Cancellation and Additional Charges) Regulations 2013, regulations 10, 12 and Schedules 2 and 3). Failing to give the cancellation information is a criminal offence (regulation 19) and extends the cancellation period by up to 12 months. Start work inside the 14 days only at the consumer's express request, made on a durable medium, telling them they will pay a proportionate amount if they then cancel (regulation 36). Leave when asked and never return when asked not to, which is a banned practice (DMCC Act 2024, Schedule 20); treat a no-cold-callers sign or a local no-cold-calling zone as a request not to call; no pressure, no long visits, extra care with anyone who may be vulnerable (X9 aggressive practices); every claim made at the door is evidenced (X9). Confirm with a qualified adviser.

### United States

US block: X21 below; everything else still to fill from sources. For any US point not covered, X1 applies: say the point isn't covered and confirm with a qualified adviser before acting.

- X21 [a US owner or US business setting the legal form or owner pay, renting the owner's home to the business, selling investments at a loss, giving to charity, or choosing health cover and savings] [Fixed] US owner tax items, each legal only on its conditions; never present any of them as a "hack" or as free money, and none replaces X1. (1) Owner pay in an S corporation: an owner who works in the business must first take a reasonable salary at the market rate for the work done, with payroll tax on it; only profit above that, taken as distributions, avoids employment tax, and the IRS can reclassify distributions as wages when the salary is too low. (2) Renting the owner's home to the business (Internal Revenue Code section 280A(g)): rent received for fewer than 15 days in the year is excluded from the owner's income, and the business may deduct it only for a genuine business use at a fair market rent an unrelated party would pay, with dated records of each use; inflated rent or routine use is disallowed, and one day over the limit makes all of the rent taxable. (3) Capital losses: losses on investments sold below cost first offset capital gains without limit, then up to the annual limit set by the IRS against other income, with the rest carried forward until used; they do not carry back. Stock or securities rebought within 30 days before or after the sale trigger the wash sale rule and the loss is disallowed for now; directly held crypto is outside that rule under current law, but bills to extend it were pending in late 2026, so check the law in force at the date of sale. (4) Giving to charity: shares held more than one year and given directly to a public charity or a donor-advised fund are deductible at market value without tax on the gain, for those who itemise, within the percentage-of-income limits and floors in force that year; shares held one year or less are deductible only at the lower of cost and market value. (5) Health savings accounts: open only with qualifying high-deductible or other eligible health cover; contributions are deductible, growth is untaxed, and withdrawals are tax-free only for qualified medical expenses, otherwise taxed and, before 65, penalised; keep receipts. Check every limit, threshold and date against current IRS guidance before acting, and keep each item out of M19's reinvestment and payment split until a qualified adviser has confirmed it for the owner's situation. (US, to check.) Confirm with a qualified adviser.
